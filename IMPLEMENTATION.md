## Calendar-fixing facts (read first — they fix the dates)

1. **NYSED requires PH-200 filed 10–14 weeks before the transfer** (12–16 weeks for sterile compounding). For a closing on ~Day 175, PH-200 must be filed by ~Day 77–105 — which is *during* diligence — and a signed bill of sale is required before the registration can issue, so the purchase agreement must be substantially agreed before the filing window closes. **If diligence runs long, the closing date moves, not the filing rule.**
2. **PBM recredentialing takes weeks to months** with no published guaranteed turnaround — it must start in Stage 4, not at closing, or the store will open under the new owner unable to bill.
3. **The Medicaid moratorium runs through at least 27 January 2027 and may be extended.** Stage 2 ends at a **hard pause** (see §3 below). If the moratorium is still in force at Day 60, the owner waits — using the time to complete Stages 0–1 and keep the target screen warm — and the 180-day clock restarts from Stage 3 when the gate clears. A roadmap that pretends the moratorium does not exist would be worse than useless.

### §3 — HARD PAUSE at Day 60 (read before proceeding)

- **What stops:** No letter of intent is signed, no deposit is paid, and no closing date is set until the attorney confirms in writing that the moratorium is lifted or that the intended structure is outside it.
- **What continues while paused:** Stages 0–1 work (financing pre-work, pharmacist search, target screen kept warm, lender relationships deepened, NCPA/CPANY/PSAO engagement). The 180-day clock **restarts from Stage 3** when the gate clears.
- **What happens if the gate fails at this point:** The owner waits. The correct move is to wait for a suitable target under a cleared moratorium — **not** to switch to a startup, which is equally caught for a new enrollment. Re-check the moratorium position quarterly. If an entity-purchase structure that preserves the existing enrollment becomes available at a fair price while the moratorium is extended indefinitely, that structure becomes relatively more attractive — but only with escrow sized to the audit exposure and counsel's written confirmation of the enrolment position (Master Report §9.7 flip table).

**Go / No-Go — Day 60:**
- **GO** only if the updated written opinion clears Gate 1. Proceed to Stage 3.
- **NO-GO (pause)** if the moratorium is in force and the intended structure is caught. Stop the clock; do not sign anything.

## If the flip condition triggers — the startup-branch roadmap (summary)

If no suitable target is available at a U.S.-multiple price after a genuine search (§9.7), **and** the owner has a specific underserved location or prescriber relationship plus capital for an 18–24-month ramp, the 180 days look different:

| Period | Work |
|---|---|
| Days 1–30 | Team + site selection in the §4.3 top neighbourhoods + lease negotiation |
| Days 31–60 | Lease signed, contractor quotes, build-out begins, PH-200 filed (10–14 weeks before planned opening) |
| Days 61–120 | Build-out, fixtures, software selection and implementation (§7.8), hiring (PIC + RPhTs + front-store), wholesaler account, insurance |
| Days 121–150 | NYSED inspection, DEA, NPI, Medicare/Medicaid enrollment (**moratorium applies**), PBM/PSAO credentialing from zero |
| Days 151–180 | Soft opening, prescriber outreach, patient-transfer campaign, first immunisation clinic (if certified) |

The 180 days end at **opening**, not at revenue maturity — the startup then faces the 18–24-month ramp in §5.4, with Year 1 EBITDA-negative in every scenario (Lean −$76,031; Standard −$56,315; Larger −$160,701, base case), and with dispensing losing $0.43 per prescription at the national average ($14.57 gross profit vs. $15.00 cost to dispense, 2024) until front-end and clinical services carry the profit. **Do not use the startup branch as a fallback for a failed acquisition search unless both conditions above (specific location/prescriber flow + ramp capital) are met; otherwise the correct move is to wait** (§9.7, "do neither yet").

Startup capital for reference (not a budget for any specific site): Lean **$435,230** / Standard **$791,010** / Larger **$1,309,273** (§5.2); break-even 68 / 111 / 171 Rx/day (§5.5); monthly EBITDA positive in months 10 / 8 / 9 of Year 1 (§1.4).
