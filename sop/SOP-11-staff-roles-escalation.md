**Source of truth:** `Pharmacy_Startup_Advisory_Brooklyn_Master_Report_2026-11-09.md` (9 Nov 2026). All figures as of that date; re-confirm with the named agency before relying on them. This SOP is general operational guidance, not legal advice.

Commit no capital until all five gates clear: (1) Medicaid moratorium resolved in writing; (2) suitable target at a disciplined price; (3) supervising pharmacist secured in writing; (4) financing confirmed without SBA; (5) healthcare attorney + pharmacy CPA engaged pre-LOI.
**Owner:** the prospective owner is a U.S. Green Card holder (Lawful Permanent Resident), NOT Bhupesh. The owner may own 100% of the pharmacy without a pharmacist licence (Ed. Law §6808).
**Hard lines enforced in this SOP:**
- **The owner NEVER dispenses, verifies prescriptions, receives oral prescriptions, interprets a prescription, counsels patients, or performs any pharmacist-only duty.** The owner manages the business; the PIC manages the practice of pharmacy. Neither directs the other's domain: the owner never overrules the PIC on a professional matter, and the PIC never assigns their responsibility to others.
- **Both owner and PIC are jointly responsible for compliance** (Ed. Law §6808(2)(c)). The PIC "may not assign his/her responsibility to others."
- **SBA is CLOSED to this owner:** SBA Policy Notice 5000-876441 (eff. 1 Mar 2026, now SOP 50 10 8.1) — a green card holder may not own any share of a 7(a)/504/Microloan borrower, even 1%.
- **NY Medicaid moratorium:** CMS-approved 27 Jul 2026, through 27 Jan 2027, extendable in 6-month periods; covers new enrollments AND changes of ownership. Do not count Medicaid revenue until enrollment is active.

## Purpose

State plainly who does what, who decides what, who is called when something goes wrong, and how absence and misconduct are handled — so that no task falls between two people, no unlicensed person does licensed work, and no absence leaves the pharmacy trading without a pharmacist.

## Scope

All roles in the pharmacy: owner, PIC, staff pharmacists, RPhTs, pharmacy interns, front-store staff, delivery driver/courier, bookkeeper, and outside CPA/attorney. Covers duties, prohibitions, supervision ratios, escalation paths, absence cover, and disciplinary/incident escalation. Staffing numbers by phase are planning guidance in the master report (§7.4) — this SOP states the rules that bind any roster.

## Owner role (who is accountable)

- **Owner (accountable for the business):** business strategy, lease, capital, banking, vendor/wholesaler contracts (with PIC/CPA input), hiring (with PIC input on pharmacy roles), marketing, front-store merchandising, delivery operations, and oversight of the CPA/bookkeeper. The owner is the employer of record: Form I-9 for each hire, tax withholding, workers' compensation, disability, and Paid Family Leave insurance, and NY wage/hour compliance. E-Verify is not required for a private NY pharmacy employer as a matter of NY law (federal contractors excepted) — confirm with employment counsel.
- The owner never counsels, verifies, dispenses, receives oral prescriptions, or supervises pharmacy practice — even when short-staffed.

### Supervision ratio — verify before rostering

**[CONFIRM WITH COUNSEL/CPA]:** the master report states the amended Regents rule as no more than **2 RPhTs per pharmacist on duty**, or **4 unlicensed persons** in non-licensure activities, with the **total not exceeding 4 individuals at one time** (interns exempt from the ratio but supervised under Part 63) — but the underlying text (8 NYCRR §63.14 / §29.7(a)(21), as amended under Chapter 543, Laws of 2025, effective 29 Apr 2026) must be confirmed in writing with NYSED/counsel at the point of hiring. **Do not roster more than 2 RPhTs per pharmacist on duty until that ratio is confirmed in writing.** RPhTs may now practise in any registered pharmacy (Chapter 543 removed the old Article 28 confinement) — confirm the current text of §63.14 and Ed. Law §6841 with NYSED/counsel at hiring and keep the confirmation on file.

### Roles the owner must not combine (from the master report)

The owner (if not a pharmacist) must not counsel, verify, receive oral prescriptions, or supervise pharmacy practice. The bookkeeper must not dispense, handle controlled-substance ordering/receipt alone, or reconcile the controlled-substance perpetual inventory. The driver must not counsel, take new prescription orders by phone, or decide substitutions. Write these prohibitions into every job description.

## Prerequisites

1. Every role has a written job description stating its duties, its prohibitions, its supervisor, and its escalation path. The employee signs it on hire.
2. Every employee signs the SOP manual on hire and on each material change, and completes training on hire and annually (SOP-08).
3. The PIC employment agreement covers duties, hours (30+ hrs/week), and PIC-succession provisions. **[CONFIRM WITH COUNSEL/CPA]:** non-compete/non-solicit enforceability in NY, termination terms, and wage/hour compliance — confirm with employment counsel before signing.
4. A relief-pharmacist pool is identified before opening. Never open without a pharmacist (SOP-01).

## Numbered steps — escalation paths

1. **Clinical question from a patient (any staff member):** fetch the pharmacist immediately. Say, "Let me get the pharmacist for you." Do not answer it yourself. Log the handoff if the pharmacist directs it.
2. **Suspect prescription, DUR alert, or PMP concern:** hold the prescription, escalate to the pharmacist on duty, then to the PIC the same day (SOP-02/SOP-09/SOP-10).
3. **Dispensing error or near-miss:** stop, contain (do not release / contact the patient if already released — SOP-02), notify the PIC the same day, and log it. The PIC decides on prescriber contact, patient contact, and any reporting, with counsel's advice.
4. **Controlled-substance variance, loss, or suspected diversion:** notify the PIC and the owner the same day; follow SOP-10 (investigate the same week; Form 106 per counsel-confirmed timing).
5. **PBM audit, OMIG/MFCU letter, or Board inspection:** notify the PIC and the owner the same day, then the attorney the same day (SOP-04/SOP-08). Do not respond to OMIG/MFCU without counsel. Alter no records.
6. **Cash discrepancy above the threshold:** notify the owner/PIC the same day and investigate before the next trading day (SOP-05).
7. **Security incident, robbery, or break-in:** people first (SOP-09, step 15), then police, owner, and PIC as soon as safe. Preserve CCTV.
8. **HIPAA breach suspected:** notify the PIC and the attorney the same day; follow the breach-response plan (SOP-08). Do not delete or alter records.
9. **Any staff member asked to do work outside their licensed scope:** decline, fetch the pharmacist/PIC, and log the request if it recurs. The PIC/owner review recurring requests and retrain or reassign.
10. **Disagreement between owner and PIC on a professional matter:** the PIC's professional judgment governs the practice of pharmacy. The owner records the disagreement, both sign the note, and the attorney is consulted if it cannot be resolved. The owner never directs the PIC to dispense, verify, or counsel against the PIC's judgment.

## Numbered steps — absence cover

11. **PIC absent (planned):** a named relief/cover pharmacist is rostered, the PIC hands over in writing (open issues, controlled-substance position, pending audits, clinic schedule), and NYSED is notified if the PIC changes (7 days, Form PH-205 — SOP-08).
12. **PIC absent (unplanned, e.g. sick):** call the relief pool immediately. If no licensed pharmacist can cover, do NOT open the pharmacy area (SOP-01). Notify patients with promised pickups by phone. Log the closure.
13. **Staff pharmacist absent:** the PIC re-rosters within the supervision ratio (see above). If the roster would exceed the ratio or leave the pharmacy without a pharmacist, reduce hours or close the pharmacy area — do not stretch the ratio.
14. **RPhT / front-store / driver absent:** cover from the trained pool or reduce the affected service (e.g. pause deliveries) — never cover a pharmacist-only task with a non-pharmacist.
15. **Owner absent:** the PIC runs the pharmacy; the bookkeeper/CPA hold the business reporting. The owner remains contactable for same-day incident notification. No incident waits for the owner's return.

## Numbered steps — disciplinary and incident escalation

16. **Minor lapse (e.g. missed log entry, late count):** PIC speaks to the person the same day, records the conversation, retrains on the SOP step, and re-checks the following week.
17. **Repeated lapse or a lapse that creates patient, audit, or financial risk:** written warning from the PIC/owner, a retraining plan with a date, and a re-audit. Record the warning in the personnel file.
18. **Serious misconduct (e.g. working outside licensed scope, falsifying a record, theft, diversion, sharing a login, filling a suspect prescription):** suspend the relevant duties immediately, notify the owner and the attorney the same day, preserve records and CCTV, and follow counsel's advice before any further step. Do not investigate a colleague alone and do not confront a suspected person without the PIC/attorney's direction.
19. **PIC misconduct or PIC departure:** the owner notifies the attorney the same day, secures the controlled-substance inventory with another licensed pharmacist, and notifies NYSED within 7 days if the PIC changes. Do not trade without a named PIC.
20. **Every disciplinary step is documented** (date, facts, who was present, action taken, review date) and filed in the personnel file. Verbal warnings are confirmed in writing.

## Controls (what prevents error)

- Written job descriptions with prohibitions, signed on hire.
- Supervision ratio enforced on every roster (2 RPhTs per pharmacist max until confirmed — see above).
- Relief pool identified before opening; default is closed, not trading without a pharmacist.
- Escalation paths posted where staff can see them; same-day notification for every serious incident.
- PIC's professional judgment governs clinical matters; owner never overrules it.
- Discipline is documented, proportionate, and counsel-advised for serious matters.

## Failure modes (what goes wrong and the response)

| Failure | Response |
|---|---|
| No pharmacist available for an open hour | Close the pharmacy area. Do not trade without a pharmacist. Notify patients and log the closure. |
| Roster would exceed the RPhT ratio | Re-roster within the ratio or reduce service. Never exceed the ratio "just for one shift." |
| Non-pharmacist performs a pharmacist-only task | Stop the task, fetch the pharmacist, log the incident, and retrain. Repeat occurrences trigger step 17–18. |
| PIC and owner disagree on a clinical decision | PIC's judgment governs. Record the disagreement, both sign, and consult the attorney if unresolved. |
| Relief pharmacist is unfamiliar with the SOPs | PIC briefs them before the shift and checks their licence. Do not let an unbriefed pharmacist verify or counsel. |
| Staff member reports a concern about a colleague | Handle through the confidential route (SOP-09). Protect the reporter from retaliation. PIC/owner + counsel decide the investigation. |
| PIC leaves with no successor | Notify NYSED within 7 days, secure controlled substances with another licensed pharmacist, and do not trade without a named PIC. Recruit urgently; if no PIC can be secured, do neither (no startup, no acquisition). |
