# SOP-08 — Compliance Monitoring

**Source of truth:** `Pharmacy_Startup_Advisory_Brooklyn_Master_Report_2026-11-09.md` (9 Nov 2026). All figures as of that date; re-confirm with the named agency before relying on them. This SOP is general operational guidance, not legal advice.
**Verdict carried forward:** CONDITIONAL GO — ACQUIRE, DO NOT BUILD. Commit no capital until all five gates clear: (1) Medicaid moratorium resolved in writing; (2) suitable target at a disciplined price; (3) supervising pharmacist secured in writing; (4) financing confirmed without SBA; (5) healthcare attorney + pharmacy CPA engaged pre-LOI.
**Owner:** the prospective owner is a U.S. Green Card holder (Lawful Permanent Resident), NOT Bhupesh. The owner may own 100% of the pharmacy without a pharmacist licence (Ed. Law §6808).
**Hard lines enforced in this SOP:**
- **The owner NEVER dispenses, verifies prescriptions, counsels patients, or performs pharmacist-only duties.** The owner funds compliance, reads the reports, and hires the attorney/CPA — the PIC owns the compliance programme.
- **Both owner and PIC are jointly responsible for compliance** (Ed. Law §6808(2)(c)). The owner cannot contract this liability away to the PIC.
- **SBA is CLOSED to this owner:** SBA Policy Notice 5000-876441 (eff. 1 Mar 2026, now SOP 50 10 8.1) — a green card holder may not own any share of a 7(a)/504/Microloan borrower, even 1%.
- **NY Medicaid moratorium:** CMS-approved 27 Jul 2026, through 27 Jan 2027, extendable in 6-month periods; covers new enrollments AND changes of ownership. Do not count Medicaid revenue until enrollment is active.

## Purpose

Keep every licence, registration, enrolment, policy, and record current and provable — so that an inspection, a PBM audit, or an OMIG/MFCU letter never finds the pharmacy unready, and so that every deadline is met before it expires.

## Scope

Licence/registration tracking, the compliance calendar, HIPAA, OSHA, inspections, the monthly self-audit, training, record retention, and audit readiness. Covers NYSED, DEA, NPI/NCPDP, Medicare/Medicaid, PBM re-credentialing, DSCSA, PMP, immunisation certification, and insurance renewals. Detailed procedures live in the SOPs they serve (SOP-04 for PBM audits, SOP-10 for DEA, SOP-07 for immunisations).

## Owner role (who is accountable)

- **Owner (accountable):** funds the compliance programme, engages the attorney and CPA before opening (not after the first audit letter), reads the PIC's monthly self-audit, and ensures renewals are filed on time. The owner signs the registration and renewal applications with the PIC.
- **PIC (responsible):** owns the compliance programme day to day — the compliance manual, the calendar, the monthly self-audit, training, and inspection readiness.
- **All staff:** complete training on hire and annually, sign the attendance record, and follow the SOPs. No staff member works outside their licensed scope (SOP-11).

## Prerequisites

1. A written compliance manual exists (this SOP set), is reviewed at least annually by the PIC and the attorney, and is signed by every employee on hire and on each material change.
2. A compliance calendar is posted/maintained with every renewal, reporting, and training deadline (see step 1).
3. The attorney and CPA are engaged and their contact details are posted where the PIC and owner can reach them the same day.
4. Insurance is bound before opening: professional liability (pharmacist), general liability, property, business interruption, cyber/privacy, workers' compensation, and (if delivery is offered) commercial auto / non-owned auto. Confirm coverages and limits with a broker — no premium is stated in the master report.

## Numbered steps

1. **Maintain the compliance calendar.** Enter and diary every item below, with the responsible person and the deadline. Review the calendar at the PIC/owner meeting each month.
   - NYSED pharmacy registration renewal — triennial ($260 renewal; $345 initial; $50 relocation endorsement). File before expiry.
   - Pharmacist licence renewals (PIC and staff pharmacists) and RPhT triennial registrations ($100 each triennial registration; initial RPhT $175 = $75 licence + $100 registration).
   - DEA registration renewal (Form 224a; $888/3 years).
   - NPI/NCPDP maintenance (NPI free; NCPDP first-ID fee **[CONFIRM WITH COUNSEL/CPA]** — UNVERIFIED, see SOP-04).
   - Medicare (PECOS) and NY Medicaid (eMedNY/NYRx) enrolment maintenance; revalidation when required.
   - PBM network re-credentialing (per each PBM/PSAO agreement).
   - DSCSA readiness checks (SOP-03).
   - PMP/I-STOP enrolment (Health Commerce System) and 24-hour dispensing reporting.
   - Immunisation-certification renewal (approved course within the past 3 years + current CPR/BLS).
   - Insurance renewals (all policies in prerequisite 4).
   - NY Medicaid compliance-program certification (eMedNY-490602) — **[CONFIRM WITH COUNSEL/CPA]:** whether a small independent pharmacy needs a full OMIG compliance program or a scaled program, and what it must contain, should be confirmed with a healthcare compliance attorney (master report §3.5, item 9).
2. **Report changes on time.**
   - PIC change: notify NYSED within **7 days** (Form PH-205, Regents Rule 29.7(a)(10)) and apply for an amended registration.
   - Ownership/officer changes: Form PB327 — **[CONFIRM WITH COUNSEL/CPA]:** the exact ownership-change reporting deadline is UNVERIFIED in the master report (secondary materials cite 30 days after the change; the statute states no day count) — confirm the deadline and form with the Board of Pharmacy before relying on 30 days.
   - Location change / relocation: NYSED relocation endorsement ($50) and a new registration for the new address (registration is address-specific).
   - Post-closing notices after an acquisition: PB327, NPPES update, and Medicare change-of-ownership report — each within 30 days where the master report's acquisition roadmap states 30 days; confirm each deadline with the attorney at closing.
3. **Run the monthly self-audit.** The PIC conducts a documented monthly self-audit covering: prescription records, controlled-substance perpetual inventory, signature logs, PMP checks, DSCSA data, expiry, temperature logs, and cash reconciliation. Record findings, actions, and completion dates. The owner reads and signs the self-audit each month.
4. **Train on hire and annually.** Mandatory training for all relevant staff: HIPAA privacy and security, fraud/waste/abuse, controlled-substance handling, DSCSA, immunisation (for immunisers), and this pharmacy's own SOPs. Keep signed attendance records. No one works unsupervised on a task they have not been trained for.
5. **HIPAA — operate as a covered entity.** Apply minimum-necessary access, role-based logins (never shared), workstation locks, encrypted backups, and a business-associate agreement (BAA) with every vendor that touches protected health information (PMS, PBM, wholesaler, courier, billing). Maintain a written breach-response plan and follow it if a breach is suspected — notify the PIC and the attorney the same day.
6. **OSHA — operate a safe workplace.** Maintain a hazard-communication program with safety data sheets (SDSs), an Exposure Control Plan before immunisations begin, sharps containers, and required training at hire and annually. **[CONFIRM WITH COUNSEL/CPA]:** confirm the exact OSHA programme contents for this pharmacy with counsel/CPA before opening.
7. **BNE licence — confirm in writing.** **[CONFIRM WITH COUNSEL/CPA]:** confirm with the NYSDOH Bureau of Narcotic Enforcement whether this specific retail pharmacy (no automated dispensing system, no institutional contracts) needs any DOH-4330 licence. The master report's position is that a BNE licence is NOT a standard step for ordinary retail dispensing, but one secondary source asserts otherwise — get BNE's written answer before opening and file it.
8. **Prepare for inspections.** Keep the pharmacy clean and organised, the SOPs accessible, training records filed, and the PIC present for any Board of Pharmacy inspection. The State Board of Pharmacy and the Department may enter and inspect the pharmacy, records, equipment, and materials at reasonable times (Ed. Law §6808(6)). Do not obstruct an inspection; notify the attorney the same day an inspection occurs.
9. **Retain records per counsel's instruction.** Prescription records, purchase/dispensing records, controlled-substance records (≥2 years, 21 CFR Part 1304), DSCSA transaction data (6 years), PBM remittances and audit correspondence, and tax records. **[CONFIRM WITH COUNSEL/CPA]:** the exact NY prescription-record retention period was NOT verified in the master report — confirm with counsel and do not assume a period. Do not discard any record that is the subject of an audit, inspection, or legal hold.
10. **Review the manual annually.** The PIC and the attorney review the compliance manual (this SOP set) at least annually and after any material legal, payer, or operational change. Record the review date and the changes made. Every employee signs the updated manual.

## Controls (what prevents error)

- One compliance calendar, reviewed monthly by PIC and owner — no deadline lives only in one person's head.
- Monthly documented self-audit, signed by PIC and owner.
- Training on hire and annually, with signed attendance records.
- Change-reporting deadlines diarised (PIC 7 days; ownership/location per counsel-confirmed deadlines).
- BAAs with every vendor touching PHI; role-based logins; encrypted backups; breach-response plan.
- No record discarded while under audit, inspection, or legal hold.

## Failure modes (what goes wrong and the response)

| Failure | Response |
|---|---|
| A renewal deadline is missed | Notify the PIC, owner, and attorney the same day. File immediately, log the miss, and add a second reminder to the calendar. Do not trade on an expired registration. |
| PIC leaves and no replacement is named | Notify NYSED within 7 days (Form PH-205). Do not open the pharmacy area without a named PIC (SOP-01). Recruit urgently; if no PIC can be secured, do not trade. |
| Inspection finds a deficiency | Record the finding, notify the attorney the same day, correct it within the stated deadline, and log the correction. Do not alter records after the inspection. |
| Suspected HIPAA breach | Follow the breach-response plan, notify the PIC and the attorney the same day, and do not delete or alter affected records. |
| BNE answer contradicts the plan | Follow BNE's written answer, notify the attorney, and update this SOP and the licensing checklist before opening. |
| Self-audit finds repeated documentation failures | PIC retrains, changes the SOP step that failed, and re-audits the following month. Repeated failures trigger SOP-11 escalation. |
