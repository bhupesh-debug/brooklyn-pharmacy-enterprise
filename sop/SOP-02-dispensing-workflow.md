# SOP-02 — Dispensing Workflow: Prescription Intake Through Pickup

**Source of truth:** `Pharmacy_Startup_Advisory_Brooklyn_Master_Report_2026-11-09.md` (9 Nov 2026). All figures as of that date; re-confirm with the named agency before relying on them. This SOP is general operational guidance, not legal advice.

**Owner:** the prospective owner is a U.S. Green Card holder (Lawful Permanent Resident), NOT Bhupesh. The owner may own 100% of the pharmacy without a pharmacist licence (Ed. Law §6808).
**Hard lines enforced in this SOP:**
- **The owner NEVER dispenses, verifies prescriptions, receives oral prescriptions, interprets a prescription, or counsels patients on prescription therapy.** Those are pharmacist-only (or pharmacist/intern) tasks. The owner manages the business, not the practice of pharmacy.
- **Final verification and release of every prescription is pharmacist-only.** A registered pharmacy technician (RPhT) may assist only under direct personal supervision on tasks requiring no professional judgment.
- **SBA is CLOSED to this owner:** SBA Policy Notice 5000-876441 (eff. 1 Mar 2026, now SOP 50 10 8.1) — a green card holder may not own any share of a 7(a)/504/Microloan borrower, even 1%.
- **NY Medicaid moratorium:** CMS-approved 27 Jul 2026, through 27 Jan 2027, extendable in 6-month periods; covers new enrollments AND changes of ownership. Do not bill Medicaid until enrollment is active; NYRx enrolment must be complete before the first Medicaid prescription is billed.
- **Economics warning (NCPA 2026 Digest, verified):** $14.57 gross profit per Rx vs $15.00 cost to dispense (−$0.43/Rx); 20.9% margin. Dispensing alone loses money at the national average — know the PBM rate before filling (SOP-04) and do not assume volume fixes a below-cost fill.

## Purpose

Move every prescription from intake to the patient's hands through one controlled, auditable path, with a pharmacist's verification at every point where professional judgment is required, and with no prescription released unverified.

## Scope

All prescriptions: e-prescriptions, written prescriptions, oral/phone prescriptions, refills, transfers, and controlled substances (with SOP-10). Covers intake, data entry, drug utilisation review (DUR), adjudication, filling, final verification, counselling offer, will-call, pickup, and return-to-stock. Immunisations are SOP-07; delivery is SOP-06; PBM adjudication detail is SOP-04.

## Owner role (who is accountable)

- **Owner (accountable):** funds and staffs the workflow, ensures the PIC has authority over all clinical/professional decisions, and never directs a clinical decision or overrules the PIC on a professional matter. The owner does NOT touch any step marked pharmacist-only.
- **PIC (responsible):** owns the dispensing workflow, trains and supervises all staff, performs or delegates final verification only to a licensed pharmacist, and reviews the error/near-miss log.
- **Staff pharmacist(s):** verify, counsel, receive oral prescriptions, resolve clinical rejections, and contact prescribers.
- **RPhT(s):** assist under direct personal supervision only — receive written/electronic prescriptions, type labels, key data, retrieve stock, count, label, and hand over completed prescriptions while advising the patient that pharmacist counselling is available. An RPhT must NOT interpret a prescription, evaluate it for legal conformance/authenticity/interactions, receive oral prescriptions, or counsel patients.
- **Front-store staff:** take walk-in prescription drop-offs for the pharmacist's review only; answer the phone from a script; never give clinical advice — say verbatim, "Let me get the pharmacist for you."

## Prerequisites

1. A licensed pharmacist is on duty (SOP-01). No pharmacist = no dispensing.
2. Pharmacy management system (PMS), e-prescribing (Surescripts), PBM switch, and PMP/I-STOP access are live and tested.
3. Staff are trained on this SOP and have signed the training record; RPhTs hold NY registration (see SOP-11 for the ratio rule).
4. PBM/PSAO contracts and the NYRx enrolment position are known before billing any claim (SOP-04; moratorium gate above).

## Numbered steps

1. **Intake — e-prescription.** Receive into the PMS via Surescripts. A pharmacist/intern reviews the e-prescription and initials acceptance before it enters the fill queue (8 NYCRR §63.14(b)(3)(i)). Do not queue an unreviewed e-prescription.
2. **Intake — written prescription.** Check completeness (patient, prescriber, drug, strength, quantity, directions, date, signature). Hold incomplete or suspect prescriptions for the pharmacist — never fill on assumption. Verify a new prescriber (DEA/NPI) per SOP-09.
3. **Intake — oral/phone prescription.** Pharmacist-only. The pharmacist takes the order, reads it back, and records it. Technicians and front-store staff never receive oral prescriptions.
4. **Intake — refill request.** Check refills remaining, last fill date, days' supply, prescriber authorisation, and "too soon" / duplicate-therapy flags in the PMS. If any flag fires, hold for the pharmacist.
5. **Patient record.** Create or verify the patient record using two identifiers at every handoff. Check allergies, full medication list, and date of birth before proceeding.
6. **Data entry.** RPhT keys the prescription data. The system runs prospective DUR: drug–drug and drug–allergy interactions, duplicate therapy, age/sex appropriateness, dose-range checks, and controlled-substance history.
7. **Pharmacist verification of data (before dispensing).** The pharmacist verifies ALL information needed to fill the prescription before dispensing — mandatory, not optional — and records the verification in the system. Resolve every DUR alert or document the pharmacist's reasoned override; never override silently.
8. **Controlled substances.** Check the NY PMP/I-STOP record before dispensing every controlled-substance prescription (SOP-10). Document the check, the result, and the pharmacist's decision. Schedule II: no refills. Escalate any suspicion of forgery, alteration, diversion, or "doctor shopping" to the PIC the same day.
9. **Insurance adjudication.** Submit the claim in real time through the switch (SOP-04). Work rejections the same day where possible. If the drug is not covered or the patient is uninsured, the pharmacist (not the cashier) explains the cash price and the option to ask the prescriber about an alternative. Check the adjudicated rate against acquisition cost before filling a below-cost drug — the pharmacist/owner decides whether to fill and documents the decision.
10. **Filling.** RPhT retrieves stock first-expired, first-out (FEFO), counts/measures, labels, and stages the prescription. Use barcode scanning at fill where the system supports it; scanning aids but never replaces the pharmacist's check.
11. **Final product verification — pharmacist-only.** The pharmacist checks drug, strength, dosage form, quantity, label, and underlying prescription against the filled product, then releases it. No prescription leaves the pharmacy without this step.
12. **Counselling offer.** Offer counselling by a pharmacist/intern on every new prescription (and on refills where the pharmacist judges it needed). Document the offer and the counselling given, or the patient's refusal. Federal OBRA '90 counselling standards apply to Medicaid patients; document to the PBM audit standard (SOP-07).
13. **Will-call / pickup.** File filled prescriptions in will-call with patient name and promised pickup date visible only to staff. At pickup, check two identifiers, collect the copay/cash as adjudicated at the POS, repeat the counselling offer if the prescription is new, and obtain a signature/proof-of-receipt where the PBM or this SOP requires it (missing signature logs are a leading PBM audit denial point).
14. **Unclaimed prescriptions.** Follow up on a documented schedule. Return unclaimed prescriptions to stock with a full audit trail and reverse the PBM claim at the same time — a prescription returned to stock but not reversed is a billing error (SOP-04).
15. **Post-dispensing records.** Every step leaves an electronic audit trail (who did what, when). Log every error and near-miss the same day for PIC review (see Failure modes).

## Controls (what prevents error)

- Pharmacist initials e-prescription acceptance; pharmacist verifies all data before dispensing; pharmacist-only final verification and release.
- Two-identifier check at intake, pickup, and delivery handoff.
- DUR runs on every prescription; alerts require a documented pharmacist decision.
- Barcode scanning at fill and verification where supported.
- FEFO stock rotation; expiry checked on receipt and monthly on the shelf (SOP-03).
- Claim billed only when the prescription is dispensed, and (for will-call) only when picked up or delivered with proof of receipt.
- RPhT scope enforced: no professional-judgment tasks, no oral prescriptions, no counselling.

## Failure modes (what goes wrong and the response)

| Failure | Response |
|---|---|
| Wrong drug / strength / quantity discovered at final verification | Stop. Do not release. Correct, re-verify, log as a near-miss, and review the cause with the PIC the same day. |
| Wrong drug reaches the patient | PIC takes charge immediately: contact the patient, advise them not to take it, arrange return/correction, notify the prescriber if needed, log the incident, and follow counsel's advice on any reporting. Do not conceal or alter the record. |
| DUR alert overridden without documentation | Treat as a near-miss. PIC reviews with the pharmacist involved and retrains; repeat overrides trigger SOP-11 escalation. |
| Prescription is suspect (forgery/alteration) | Hold it. Pharmacist calls the prescriber to verify. Do not fill. Log and escalate to the PIC the same day (SOP-09). |
| Claim rejected and patient is waiting | Pharmacist explains the position, works the rejection (PA, formulary alternative via prescriber), and documents the outcome. Cashier never promises a price the pharmacist has not confirmed. |
| Patient refuses counselling | Document the refusal. Dispense as verified. Do not argue or record counselling that did not happen. |
| Unclaimed prescription returned to stock, claim not reversed | Reverse the claim immediately on discovery, log the correction, and add a same-day reconciliation check to the daily close (SOP-04). |
| Owner or non-pharmacist asked to verify/counsel | Decline and fetch the pharmacist. The owner never performs a pharmacist-only task, even "just this once." Log the request if it recurs, for PIC/owner review. |
