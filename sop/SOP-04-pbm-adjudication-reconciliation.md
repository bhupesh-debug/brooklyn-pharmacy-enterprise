# SOP-04 — PBM Adjudication, Reconciliation, and Audit Preparation

**Source of truth:** `Pharmacy_Startup_Advisory_Brooklyn_Master_Report_2026-11-09.md` (9 Nov 2026). All figures as of that date; re-confirm with the named agency before relying on them. This SOP is general operational guidance, not legal advice.
**Verdict carried forward:** CONDITIONAL GO — ACQUIRE, DO NOT BUILD. Commit no capital until all five gates clear: (1) Medicaid moratorium resolved in writing; (2) suitable target at a disciplined price; (3) supervising pharmacist secured in writing; (4) financing confirmed without SBA; (5) healthcare attorney + pharmacy CPA engaged pre-LOI.
**Owner:** the prospective owner is a U.S. Green Card holder (Lawful Permanent Resident), NOT Bhupesh. The owner may own 100% of the pharmacy without a pharmacist licence (Ed. Law §6808).
**Hard lines enforced in this SOP:**
- **The owner NEVER dispenses, verifies prescriptions, or performs pharmacist-only duties.** The owner may review contracts and remittances commercially with the attorney/CPA, but clinical rejection decisions and prescriber contacts are the pharmacist's.
- **SBA is CLOSED to this owner:** SBA Policy Notice 5000-876441 (eff. 1 Mar 2026, now SOP 50 10 8.1) — a green card holder may not own any share of a 7(a)/504/Microloan borrower, even 1%.
- **NY Medicaid moratorium:** CMS-approved 27 Jul 2026, through 27 Jan 2027, extendable in 6-month periods; covers new enrollments AND changes of ownership. Do not bill Medicaid until enrollment is active.
- **Economics (NCPA 2026 Digest, verified):** $14.57 gross profit per Rx vs $15.00 cost to dispense (−$0.43/Rx); 20.9% margin. The Big 3 PBMs (CVS Caremark, Express Scripts, OptumRx) manage ~79% of U.S. prescriptions (FTC, Jul 2024) — the pharmacy is a price-taker. Know the rate before filling. **Break-even (base case, $79.20/Rx, 20.9% margin, master report §5.5): 68 Rx/day (Lean) / 111 Rx/day (Standard) / 171 Rx/day (Larger).** Margin moves break-even more than revenue per Rx does — at 18.2% margin, break-even rises to 78 / 127 / 194 Rx/day.
- **DIR:** retroactive pharmacy DIR for Part D ended 1 Jan 2024 (CMS rule, 87 Fed. Reg. 27,704) — price concessions are now at point of sale. But PBMs lowered baseline rates, and **GER/BER reconciliations under commercial contracts can still be retroactive**. Do not assume "DIR is over" means no retroactive adjustment can happen.

## Purpose

Submit every claim correctly, get paid what the contract promises, reconcile every remittance claim-by-claim, and be audit-ready on day one — because most PBM chargebacks are documentation failures, and documentation is within the pharmacy's control.

## Scope

All third-party claims: commercial, Medicare Part D, Medicare Advantage, and NYRx/Medicaid (once enrolled). Covers network enrolment checks, adjudication, rejections and prior authorisations, reversals, remittance reconciliation, GER/BER and clawback handling, MAC appeals, and PBM/OMIG audit response. Cash sales and discount cards are covered only where they interact with third-party billing (see step 9).

## Owner role (who is accountable)

- **Owner (accountable):** reads every PBM/PSAO agreement before signing (with attorney review — never sign unread), approves network enrolment decisions with the PIC/CPA, and reviews the weekly reconciliation report. The owner never signs a PBM agreement that permits unilateral rate changes without notice and without a termination right.
- **PIC (responsible):** owns adjudication quality, rejection resolution that needs judgment, and the audit-response procedure.
- **Bookkeeper / billing staff:** submit and reconcile claims, match remittances, and flag short-pays the same week. They do not resolve clinical rejections or contact prescribers about clinical matters.
- **Attorney + PSAO:** review contracts before execution; support audit responses.

## Prerequisites

1. NPI (Type 2, organisation — free via NPPES), NCPDP Provider ID, NYSED registration, and DEA registration are active. **[CONFIRM WITH COUNSEL/CPA]:** the first-NCPDP-ID fee is UNVERIFIED (NCPDP FAQ states $400 only for an *additional* ID at an existing location) — do not budget a fee figure until NCPDP confirms it.
2. Medicare enrolment (PECOS/CMS-855B, $750 for 2026) is complete before Medicaid (eMedNY) enrolment; the Medicaid $750 is waived if already paid to Medicare — budget $750 total, not $1,500 (42 CFR §455.460).
3. PBM/PSAO contracts are signed only after attorney review of: reimbursement methodology, MAC lists and appeal rights, GER/BER target/period/cap, spread pricing, audit/recoupment rights (notice, look-back, extrapolation), termination and rate-change rights, and performance/star-ratings measures. Processing times (PBM credentialing commonly cited 90–180 days) are **UNVERIFIED** estimates — confirm in each agreement.
4. The PMS produces a per-prescription gross-profit report, a reimbursement-vs-acquisition-cost report, and a PBM reconciliation report. If it cannot, do not rely on it — escalate to the PIC/owner before opening.

## Numbered steps

1. **Before enrolling in any network,** the PIC/CPA model the network's actual paid amounts for the store's expected volume and top-50 drugs. Do not enrol on a headline rate. Record the modelling decision and who approved enrolment.
2. **Submit the claim in real time** through the switch at dispensing. The adjudication response is a promise to pay a stated amount if the claim is valid — it is not payment.
3. **Check the adjudicated rate against acquisition cost before filling** a drug whose reimbursement is below acquisition cost plus the cost to dispense. The pharmacist/owner decides whether to fill and documents the decision. Do not stock or fill a below-cost drug silently.
4. **Work rejections the same day where possible.** Billing staff prepare the rejection; the pharmacist resolves any rejection needing judgment, contacts the prescriber, or handles the prior authorisation (PA) clinical content. Log every rejection, action, and outcome.
5. **Appeal below-cost MAC prices in writing, in batches, with the wholesaler invoice attached.** The appeal deadline and evidence required are in the contract — diary them. Do not let an appeal deadline pass unnoticed.
6. **Bill only when dispensed.** A claim is billed only when the prescription is dispensed, and (for will-call) only when it is picked up or delivered with proof of receipt. Never bill for a prescription never dispensed or never picked up.
7. **Reverse promptly.** A prescription returned to stock, corrected, or never picked up must be reversed with the PBM at the same time as the return-to-stock. Check the reversal in the daily close.
8. **Collect the copay as adjudicated, every time.** A waived, discounted, or uncollected copay can breach the contract and, for federal payers, raise Anti-Kickback issues. Counsel must clear any copay-waiver or discount policy before the first use.
9. **Discount cards are not cash and not insurance.** Process a discount-card claim as its own third-party claim with its own (low) price and audit trail. Do not process a discount-card claim as a cash sale. Counsel must clear the discount-card/coupon policy before the first card is accepted.
10. **Reconcile remittances weekly, claim by claim.** The bookkeeper matches the PBM's electronic remittance advice (835) to the PMS claims and investigates every short-pay, denial, and reversal the same week. Unreconciled remittances are how underpayment is discovered months too late. Report the weekly reconciliation to the owner/PIC.
11. **Track GER/BER and other retroactive adjustments separately.** Log every GER/BER reconciliation line, its period, target, and amount. A contract with an uncapped GER reconciliation can take back money already spent on stock — flag any uncapped term to the attorney before signing.
12. **Track DIR/performance adjustments by contract.** For Part D, the concession is at point of sale (post-1 Jan 2024). For commercial contracts, log any performance-adjustment term and its reconciliation date. Never forecast revenue from a PBM reform or cost-plus offer that has not been made in writing — **[CONFIRM WITH COUNSEL/CPA]:** cost-plus availability and the Feb 2026 federal PBM-reform implementation timeline are UNVERIFIED in the master report.
13. **Prepare for audit from day one.** Keep complete, legible records for every claim: prescription, signature log, refill documentation, prescriber communications, days' supply, quantity, and purchase-vs-dispensing records. No record = no payment on audit.
14. **On receiving any audit letter (PBM, OMIG, or MFCU):** (a) notify the PIC and the owner the same day; (b) notify the attorney (and the PSAO's audit-support service, if any) the same day — **do not respond to OMIG/MFCU without counsel**; (c) alter, back-date, or create no records after the fact; (d) produce the requested records within the deadline, with a cover letter and an index; (e) appeal every recoupment in writing within the deadline, with the supporting record attached; (f) log the outcome and change the SOP if a lesson emerges. Never ignore an audit letter and never miss an appeal deadline.

## Controls (what prevents error)

- Attorney review of every PBM/PSAO contract before signing; no unilateral rate-change term without a termination right.
- Rate-vs-acquisition-cost check before filling below-cost drugs.
- Same-day rejection work and same-week remittance reconciliation.
- Reversal tied to return-to-stock in the same workflow.
- Copay collected as adjudicated, every time.
- Complete records from day one; audit-response procedure written before the first audit letter.

## Failure modes (what goes wrong and the response)

| Failure | Response |
|---|---|
| Claim short-paid vs the adjudicated amount | Flag in the weekly reconciliation, investigate the same week, and appeal in writing with the remittance and the adjudication response attached. |
| GER/BER clawback line appears on a remittance | Log it, verify it against the contract's target/period/cap, notify the PIC/owner/CPA, and appeal if the contract does not support it. Do not treat it as a routine deduction. |
| Audit letter arrives | Follow step 14 exactly. Notify PIC, owner, attorney, and PSAO the same day. |
| Missing signature log / refill documentation in an audit sample | The PBM will recoup the full claim amount. Appeal only with the actual record; if none exists, accept the lesson, log it, and fix the SOP step that failed. |
| Inventory audit shows dispensed > purchased on paper | Produce the perpetual inventory, DSCSA data, and a documented explanation for every variance *before* the audit response deadline. Escalate to the attorney. |
| Claim billed but prescription never picked up | Reverse the claim immediately, log the correction, and check the will-call/reversal link in the daily close. |
| Below-cost fill discovered after dispensing | Log it, notify the PIC/owner, and review whether the rate check (step 3) was skipped. Do not repeat the fill without a documented decision. |
