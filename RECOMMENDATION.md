**Prepared for:** the prospective owner — a U.S. Green Card holder (Lawful Permanent Resident), as owner/operator.
**The owner is not Bhupesh.** Bhupesh asked for this research on the owner's behalf.
**Source of truth:** *Pharmacy_Startup_Advisory_Brooklyn_Master_Report_2026-11-09.md* (105-page master report, research date 9 November 2026) and its companion financial model *Pharmacy_Startup_Financial_Model_Brooklyn_2026-11-09.xlsx*. ## 1. The verdict

## **CONDITIONAL GO — ACQUIRE, DO NOT BUILD; commit no capital until five gates clear.**

Buy an existing, diligenced independent pharmacy in Brooklyn **by asset purchase, at a U.S.-multiple price (2.5–3.5× adjusted EBITDA, or 2.5–4.5× Seller's Discretionary Earnings for a small owner-operated store), plus inventory at cost** — and **do not** open a pharmacy from scratch as the primary route. A startup is the fallback only if no suitable target exists at a disciplined price after a genuine search, and only if the owner has a specific underserved location or captive patient flow plus capital for an 18–24-month ramp. If any gate fails for a target, do not proceed with that target — **wait; do not default to a startup.** If the owner cannot secure a full-time supervising pharmacist at all, the answer is **do neither.**

(Master Report §1.2, §9.7, §11.1.)

| E3 | **A startup burns cash for 18–24 months from zero volume.** Startup capital: Lean **$435,230** / Standard **$791,010** / Larger **$1,309,273**. Break-even volume (base: $79.20/Rx, 20.9% margin): **68 / 111 / 171 Rx/day** (A/B/C) vs. steady-state targets of 80 / 150 / 220 Rx/day. Year 1 EBITDA (base) is negative in every scenario: −$76,031 / −$56,315 / −$160,701. | E8 | **The Medicaid moratorium gates both routes — a binary, dated fact.** NYSDOH moratorium (CMS-approved 27 Jul 2026, announced 4 Aug 2026) applies to new enrollments, reinstatements, and changes of ownership for pharmacies; initial period through **27 Jan 2027**, extendable in six-month periods; pending applications discontinued regardless of stage. | Master Report §1.3 Finding 2, §9.2.5, §A.2. |

Enter only if the CPA's forecast shows viability *without* assuming PBM reform or a rate increase. | Master Report §8.3, §8.4 Risk 5. FTC July 2024 interim report; AMA 2026 update. |

## 3. The five gates — with the exact evidence that clears each

**All five must clear before a letter of intent is signed or a deposit paid.** If any gate cannot be met for a specific target, do not proceed with that target.

| Gate | What clears it — exact evidence required | Who provides it |
|---|---|---|
| **Gate 1 — Medicaid moratorium resolved** | The moratorium is confirmed **lifted**, or the intended structure is confirmed **in writing by a healthcare attorney** as outside it. The written opinion must state: current status, whether an asset purchase / entity purchase / startup is caught, and what happens to a pending application if the moratorium is extended. A verbal assurance is not clearance. Re-confirm at Day 60 (hard pause — see IMPLEMENTATION.md) and again before closing. | **Healthcare/pharmacy attorney** (licensed professional), in a written opinion letter. |
| **Gate 2 — A suitable target exists at a disciplined price** | A target that meets **all** of the §9.7(b) criteria, each evidenced in diligence (§9.4): stable or growing script count over three years (monthly counts from the dispensing system, not a broker summary); gross profit per script by payer verified and positive on the material contracts; no open or recently settled PBM audit with material recoupment; no single-prescriber or single-facility concentration; a lease with at least five years remaining or renewal options the buyer controls (5 years is a judgement — no published minimum was found — labelled as such); the supervising pharmacist and key staff willing to stay; inventory verified by count; a seller willing to stay for a transition period. **And** the price is within the U.S. working range — 2.5–3.5× adjusted EBITDA (or 2.5–4.5× SDE for a small owner-operated store), plus inventory at cost, with earnings normalised line by line by the CPA (§9.3). A price above that range needs a specific, documented justification — not a broker's assertion. | **CPA / pharmacy accountant** (valuation memo with normalisation shown line by line) + **attorney** (lease, audit, compliance review) + **owner** (site visits, prescriber/patient observation). |
| **Gate 3 — Supervising pharmacist secured in writing** | A named, New York-licensed supervising pharmacist, full-time (30+ hours/week), supervising **only this one pharmacy**, confirmed **in writing** — either the incumbent staying or a recruited candidate — before any NYSED filing and before closing. The registration certificate bears their name; they must sign the PH-200 application. If the PIC changes, the Board must be notified within 7 days (Form PH-205; Regents Rule 29.7(a)(10)). | **The pharmacist** (written confirmation) + **attorney** (employment terms reviewed) + **owner** (recruitment). Licence verified via NYSED license lookup. |
| **Gate 4 — Financing confirmed without SBA** | A **written commitment letter** from a conventional bank and/or CDFI (own-funds, non-SBA products), with the equity injection and a working-capital line sized to several weeks of post-closing reimbursement delay. The stack is built from term sheets, not from the report's illustrative stacks (§6.11 — ASSUMPTIONS to be replaced by term sheets). No SBA 7(a), 504, or Microloan is available to this owner. Re-check the SBA SOP at the point of application — if the citizenship rule has changed or the owner has naturalised, re-price the stack with a 7(a) loan (10% equity, 10-year term) as the primary facility. | **Lenders** (commitment letter) + **CPA** (sources-and-uses, working-capital sizing) + **owner** (12 inputs, proof of funds, tax returns). |
| **Gate 5 — Healthcare attorney + pharmacy CPA engaged pre-LOI** | Engagement letters signed with a New York healthcare/pharmacy attorney **and** a pharmacy-savvy CPA **before** any letter of intent, lease, wholesaler agreement, or lender application. Scope must include: moratorium opinion, structure opinion, LOI/purchase-agreement review, lease review, PBM contract review (attorney); target screening, earnings normalisation, diligence review, working-capital sizing (CPA). | **Owner** selects; **attorney + CPA** engaged. |

**If any gate fails for a target: do not proceed with that target. The fallback is to wait for a suitable target — not to default to a startup. If no supervising pharmacist can be secured at all, do neither.**

## 5. What to avoid — an explicit list

Each item states what goes wrong if it is ignored. These are traps the master report names; the list is not exhaustive, but every item below is a named trap in the report.

| # | Do NOT do this | What goes wrong if it is ignored |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | **Do not pursue the SBA route.** Do not apply for an SBA 7(a), 504, Microloan, or Community Advantage loan as an owner, and do not budget on the assumption that one will be available. | The application will be refused: SBA Policy Notice 5000-876441 (eff. 1 Mar 2026) and SOP 50 10 8.1 (eff. 1 Oct 2026) bar any LPR ownership — direct or indirect, even 1% — in an SBA borrower. The owner loses months, pays application costs, and arrives at closing without the financing the plan assumed. Older sources (including SBA's own pre-2026 pages) still say LPRs are eligible — they are out of date. |
| A2 | **Do not use a nominal U.S.-citizen co-owner workaround.** Do not add a U.S. citizen as a nominal/paper owner, or restructure ownership, to satisfy the SBA rule. | The report states this "would be a serious compliance problem" (§1.3, §6.2) and that "a nominee arrangement would be fraud" (§6.2). Any co-owner structure must be designed by counsel — and a nominal citizen owner inserted to satisfy the SBA rule must not be attempted without counsel. The consequence is not a declined loan; it is a compliance and fraud exposure. |
| A3 | **Do not count Medicaid revenue before enrollment is active.** Do not forecast, budget, or price a target on Medicaid revenue until NY Medicaid (NYRx) enrollment is active for the buyer's entity and structure. | The moratorium (effective 27 Jul 2026, through at least 27 Jan 2027, extendable) covers new enrollments, reinstatements, and changes of ownership; pending applications are discontinued regardless of stage and do not automatically resume. Medicaid is roughly a fifth of an independent pharmacy's prescriptions — and a much larger share in the top-ranked Brooklyn neighbourhoods (Brownsville 53.6% Medicaid+Medicare combined; East New York 49.0%). Counting it early overstates revenue by the store's largest payer block and strands the owner with rent and payroll and no Medicaid billing. |
| A4 | **Do not run dispensing-only economics.** Do not build a forecast, a price, or an operating plan that assumes dispensing alone pays the bills. | At the national average, dispensing loses $0.43 per prescription ($14.57 gross profit vs. $15.00 cost to dispense). A store that grows volume on below-cost contracts grows its losses (the average independent filled 7,957 more prescriptions in 2024 than in 2023 and made $393 more in gross profit). The business only works if front-end and clinical services (immunisations, med sync, adherence packaging, MTM) carry profit dispensing cannot — and no fee for those services may be forecast until it is contracted in writing. |
| A5 | **Do not overpay for a target — above the 2.5–3.5× band without justification.** Do not pay above 2.5–3.5× adjusted EBITDA (or 2.5–4.5× SDE for a small owner-operated store), plus inventory at cost, without a specific, documented justification. Above all, **do not use the 4–6× EBITDA figure**. | The 4–6× figure is Canadian (PharmacyBroker.ca), not American. Using it on U.S. earnings overpays by roughly 1–2 turns of EBITDA — on $200,000 of normalised earnings, the difference between 3× and 5× is **$400,000** (§9.3). Overpaying for goodwill is unrecoverable: the premium is paid at closing and the store's cash flow cannot earn it back on 18.2–20.9% margins. |
| A6 | **Do not start before the pharmacist-in-charge is secured.** Do not file PH-200, sign a lease, or set a closing date without a named, NY-licensed, full-time (30+ hrs/week, one pharmacy only) supervising pharmacist secured in writing. | NYSED registration requires the PIC's signature and bears their name; the pharmacy must be under a licensed pharmacist's immediate supervision at all hours when open (Ed. Law §6808(2)(e)). Filing without a committed PIC is "the most common avoidable delay" (§2.4). Without a PIC the store cannot open at all — and if the PIC resigns, is ill, or loses their licence, the store cannot trade as a pharmacy until a new PIC is designated and the Board notified within 7 days. |
| A7 | **Do not sign a lease or LOI before the moratorium position is confirmed in writing.** Do not sign a letter of intent, pay a deposit, set a closing date, or sign a lease until the attorney's written moratorium opinion is in hand and still current. | Both a startup (new enrollment) and an asset purchase (new enrollment for the new entity) are squarely inside the moratorium while it runs (§9.2.5). Signing early commits the owner to a timetable and a deposit on a deal that cannot bill Medicaid — the store's largest payer block in the target neighbourhoods — and a pending application, if filed, is discontinued regardless of stage and does not automatically resume. |
| A8 | **Do not form a professional corporation (P.C.) or professional service LLC (PLLC).** Do not file a P.C. under BCL §1503 or a PLLC under LLC Law §1203 with a non-pharmacist owner. | BCL §1503(a) permits organisation only by individuals duly authorised to render the professional service; §§1507–1508 bar transfer of shares/voting power to non-licensees. The filing will be rejected and cost months (§2.5). The correct vehicles are a single-member LLC ($200 filing fee, recommended starting point — confirm with counsel/CPA) or a business corporation ($125). |
| A9 | **Do not sign a PBM/PSAO contract the owner has not read, or one that lets the PBM change rates unilaterally without notice and without a termination right.** Do not sign without attorney review of reimbursement methodology, MAC update/appeal terms, GER/BER target/reconciliation period/cap, spread pricing, audit/recoupment rights, and termination/rate-change rights. | GER/BER reconciliations are retroactive and survived the 2024 DIR rule change; an uncapped GER reconciliation can take back money the pharmacy has already spent on stock. PBM audits are "no longer episodic; they are constant" and have "evolved into a primary revenue-recovery tool" (Frier Levitt, NCPA 2025); most chargebacks are documentation failures. A contract signed unread is the mechanism by which a profitable-looking store becomes a loss-making one. |
| A10 | **Do not stock a drug whose reimbursement is below acquisition cost + the cost to dispense, and do not stock a high-cost brand/specialty drug without a confirmed authorisation and reimbursement in writing.** | A single specialty fill can be tens of thousands of dollars of inventory not reimbursed for weeks; refund delays of more than 21 days for specialty dispensing erode working capital (Pharmacy Times, June 2026). Stocking below cost on a material share of scripts is the #1 financial risk in the report (§8.4 Risk 1) and is how a store loses money on every fill. |
| A11 | **Do not buy QS/1 NRx for a new store, and do not select dispensing software on price alone.** | NRx sunsets **31 December 2027** — a new pharmacy must not buy it in 2026 (§7.8). No vendor publishes a list price; every system is "quote required." A cheap system that cannot produce a PBM reconciliation report will cost more in the first audit than it saved. Shortlist PioneerRx, PrimeRx, and Liberty (plus WinRx if the owner prefers the Outcomes portfolio); obtain written 3-year quotes from at least three and run the same demo script on each. |
| A12 | **Do not treat the Canadian valuation, the "30-day notice" transfer rule, or the old "8–12 weeks" NYSED timing as correct.** | Each was corrected in the master report's Appendix A: the 4–6× multiple is Canadian (§9.3, §A.8); there is no NYSED rule that a transfer requires only "30 days' notice" — the real rule is file PH200 **10–14 weeks** before the planned opening or transfer (12–16 weeks for sterile compounding), verified live on NYSED's application-requirements page 9 Nov 2026 (§9.2.2, §A.3); the phase 1–2 "8–12 weeks" is wrong. Using any of the three leaves the buyer ~7–11 weeks short or ~$400,000 overpaid. |
| A13 | **Do not offer inducements to patients or prescribers, waive copays, or enter prescriber/facility financial arrangements without the attorney's written clearance — and do not offer 340B, LTC, or sterile compounding without a separate feasibility and legal review.** | Federal Anti-Kickback Statute (42 U.S.C. §1320a-7b(b)) and NY law prohibit paying or receiving remuneration to induce referrals of federally reimbursable business. A copay waived, discounted, or not collected is (depending on the contract and payer) a contract breach and, for federal payers, a potential Anti-Kickback issue. LTC is a separate business inside the pharmacy (Phase 3 only, contract first); 340B has specific eligibility, registration, and audit requirements for both the covered entity and the contract pharmacy; sterile compounding needs a separate feasibility and compliance review and 12–16 weeks' NYSED lead time. |
| A14 | **Do not cut the working-capital reserve to make the total look smaller, and do not fund inventory with a credit card or a short-term loan without the CPA's advice.** | Working capital ($122,564 / $255,250 / $480,498 for Scenarios A/B/C) is "the line that kills under-capitalised pharmacies" (§5.2). The PBM 30–60-day lag vs. 14–30-day wholesaler terms means the store pays for the drug weeks before it is paid for it; without the reserve the pharmacy runs out of cash in months 4–6 even though the model later turns profitable. Do not reduce the reserve until a facility is committed in writing (§6.9). |
| A15 | **Do not relax the target screen under time pressure, and do not close on a promise to seek landlord consent.** | The screen (§9.7(b)) is the discipline that keeps the owner from buying a declining store, a store with an audit/lease defect, or a store priced at a Canadian multiple. Landlord consent to assignment (or a new longer lease) must be obtained **in writing as a closing condition** — a landlord may refuse or renegotiate where the buyer cannot show sufficient financial resources or business experience, and consent to assignment does not automatically release the seller/guarantors unless the deed says so (§9.1, §9.4D). A short remaining term or no renewal option guts the goodwill being paid for. |
| A16 | **Do not ignore an audit letter, miss an appeal deadline, or "fix" a record after an audit has started.** | The audit-response procedure (§8.3): notify the PIC and owner the same day; notify the attorney (and the PSAO's audit-support service, if any) the same day; no records altered, back-dated, or created after the fact; produce the requested records within the deadline with a cover letter and index; appeal every recoupment in writing within the deadline. For OMIG/MFCU: notify the attorney the day the letter arrives — **do not respond to OMIG/MFCU without counsel.** Ignoring an audit letter or missing an appeal deadline converts a defensible documentation point into a recoupment taken from a future remittance without warning. |

## 6. What would flip this recommendation

From Master Report §9.7. The recommendation is conditional; these are the facts that would change it.

| Changed fact | New recommendation |
|---|---|
| No suitable target is available in Brooklyn at a U.S.-multiple price after a genuine search (only declining stores, only stores priced at 4–6×, or only stores with audit/lease defects) | **Startup becomes the better route** — *only if* the owner has a specific underserved location or prescriber relationship, capital for an 18–24-month ramp, and a realistic payer-mix plan. Otherwise **do neither yet**. |
| The owner (or their household) has a strong, pre-existing local prescriber network or a captive patient flow (e.g., adjacent clinic relationship) that a new store could capture from day one | Startup's core disadvantage (zero volume) is materially reduced; re-weigh. Verify the flow is real and contracted, not assumed. |
| Medicaid moratorium is extended indefinitely **and** an entity-purchase structure that preserves the existing enrolment is available at a fair price | Acquisition by **entity purchase** becomes relatively more attractive despite the inherited-liability cost — but only with escrow sized to the audit exposure and counsel's written confirmation of the enrolment position. |
| A target's diligence reveals material PBM audit/recoupment exposure, DIR-fee under-accrual, a short lease the landlord will not extend, loss of the supervising pharmacist, or a declining script trend that the price does not reflect | **Walk away from that target.** Do not "fix it with a lower price" unless the discount fully reflects the quantified exposure — and get the quantification in writing from the accountant. |
| The buyer cannot secure a full-time NY supervising pharmacist willing to work in the store | **Do neither.** This is a hard gate for both routes in New York. |
| Lender declines the acquisition (structure, buyer profile, or target) but would fund a startup, or the owner can fund a startup from equity without debt | Re-weigh on the actual financing terms — but do not let availability of startup funding override the volume-ramp risk; a funded startup that never reaches volume still fails. |
| A target is offered as a **prescription-file-only** sale (no lease, no staff, no premises) | Treat as a hybrid: closer to a startup (new site, new licences, new credentialing) with a head start on patients. Price it as a file purchase (script/GP-based, with an earn-out), not as a going-concern multiple. |

**What "do neither yet" looks like.** Waiting is a legitimate outcome. While waiting: (i) confirm the Medicaid moratorium position quarterly; (ii) build the supervising-pharmacist relationship and the PSAO/wholesaler conversations *before* a target is found, so diligence can move fast; (iii) line up the healthcare attorney, accountant, and lender so a good target is not lost to a slow process; (iv) keep a written target screen (the §9.7(b) criteria) and do not relax it under time pressure. A first pharmacy purchase made in haste at the wrong multiple, with the wrong lease, or under a Medicaid freeze is worse than a delayed purchase. (§9.7.)
