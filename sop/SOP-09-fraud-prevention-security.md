## Purpose

Detect, prevent, and respond to fraud, theft, diversion, and security threats — prescription fraud, billing fraud, employee theft, and robbery — so that a suspicious prescription is never filled on assumption, a fraudulent claim is never billed, and an incident is reported the same day it is found.

## Scope

Prescription fraud, billing fraud, employee fraud/theft, kickbacks and inducements, discount cards/coupons, physical security (locks, alarms, CCTV, access control), robbery response, and incident reporting. Controlled-substance diversion detail is SOP-10; cash discrepancies are SOP-05; PBM audit response is SOP-04.

## Owner role (who is accountable)

- **Owner (accountable):** funds physical security, sets the confidential reporting route, ensures no inducement is offered to any patient or prescriber without the attorney's written clearance, and is notified of every incident the same day.
- **PIC (responsible):** owns fraud detection in the pharmacy, verifies suspect prescriptions, and decides whether a prescription is filled.
- **All staff:** follow the verification steps, never fill a suspect prescription on assumption, and report concerns through the confidential route without fear of retaliation. No staff member investigates a colleague alone.

## Prerequisites

1. CCTV covers the pharmacy entrance, dispensing area, safe, receiving area, and POS, and is working. Recordings are retained per counsel's instruction.
2. The pharmacy area is separately securable from the front store; controlled substances are in a securely locked cabinet/safe with pharmacist-only access.
3. Keys, alarm codes, and system passwords are issued per person, never shared, and recorded in the access register. They are revoked on the day a person leaves.
4. A confidential reporting route to the owner/PIC is posted and known to all staff.
5. The attorney's written clearance exists for any discount-card, coupon, delivery, or prescriber-outreach policy before it is used (SOP-04).

## Numbered steps

### Prescription fraud

1. **Verify every new prescriber** (DEA/NPI) before filling a first prescription from them. Record the verification.
2. **Pharmacist only: call the prescriber on any doubt** — altered quantities, unusual doses, unfamiliar prescriber, inconsistent handwriting/electronic format, or a pattern suggesting a "pill mill." Document the call, who was spoken to, and the outcome.
3. **Check the PMP** (SOP-10) for every controlled-substance prescription and for any prescription where diversion is suspected. Document the check and the pharmacist's decision.
4. **Hold, do not fill.** A suspect prescription is held for the pharmacist. Never fill a suspect prescription on assumption, and never fill it to "keep the patient happy."
5. **Escalate the same day.** Any suspicion of forgery, alteration, prescriber-identity theft, diversion, or "doctor shopping" is escalated to the PIC the same day and logged. The PIC decides whether to notify the prescriber, the Board, or law enforcement, with counsel's advice.

### Billing fraud prevention

6. **Bill only when dispensed** (SOP-04): a claim is billed only when the prescription is dispensed, and (for will-call) only when picked up or delivered with proof of receipt. Never bill for a prescription never dispensed or never picked up, never bill the wrong payer, and never upcode a product.
7. **Reconcile daily.** The daily claims report is reconciled to the dispensing log (SOP-05/SOP-04). Investigate any claim without a matching dispense the same day.
8. **Discount cards and coupons:** follow the counsel-cleared policy only. A discount-card claim is a third-party claim, not a cash sale. Never waive or reduce a federally insured patient's copay in a way that implicates the Anti-Kickback Statute or a PBM contract term without counsel's written clearance.

### Employee fraud and theft

9. **Segregate duties** (SOP-05): the person who orders a drug is not the only person who receives it; the person who handles cash does not reconcile the bank; the person who dispenses does not verify their own work; the bookkeeper who posts a payment does not approve it.
10. **Control access.** Pharmacist-only access to the safe/cabinet. Role-based system logins so every action is attributable to a person. Shared logins are prohibited.
11. **Count and reconcile.** Perpetual controlled-substance inventory with daily reconciliation (SOP-10); weekly cycle counts of high-value/high-theft items (SOP-03). Investigate every variance before write-off.
12. **Report confidentially.** Any staff member who suspects theft, falsification, or diversion reports it through the confidential route to the owner/PIC the same day. Do not confront the person suspected, do not investigate alone, and do not alter records.

### Physical security and robbery

13. **Lock the pharmacy area separately** whenever no pharmacist is on duty (SOP-01). Drugs must be inaccessible when no pharmacist is on duty (Ed. Law §6808(2)(f)).
14. **Keep the safe locked** except when a pharmacist is accessing it. Keep cash drops regular and the till low (SOP-05).
15. **If a robbery or threat occurs:** comply with the robber's demands, do not resist, do not chase, and protect people first. Activate the alarm/panic procedure only if it can be done safely. Call police and the owner/PIC as soon as it is safe. Do not touch anything until police arrive. Log the incident and preserve CCTV.
16. **After any security incident:** the PIC checks the controlled-substance inventory (SOP-10) and the prescription stock, the owner reviews CCTV and access records, and the attorney is notified the same day.

### Incident reporting (all fraud/security incidents)

17. **Report the same day.** Log every incident in the incident log: date, time, who found it, what happened, what was done, and who was notified. Notify the PIC and the owner the same day.
18. **Preserve evidence.** Do not alter, back-date, or create records after the fact. Preserve CCTV, logs, and the original prescription/record.
19. **Notify counsel and, where the PIC/attorney direct it, the Board, DEA, BNE, or law enforcement.** Follow counsel's advice on the timing and content of any external report. Do not make an external report about a colleague or a prescriber without the PIC/attorney's direction.

## Controls (what prevents error)

- Prescriber verification + pharmacist prescriber call on any doubt + PMP check.
- Hold-don't-fill rule for suspect prescriptions; same-day PIC escalation.
- Bill-only-when-dispensed; daily claims-to-dispensing reconciliation.
- Segregation of duties; pharmacist-only safe access; per-person codes/passwords, revoked on departure.
- CCTV over entrance, dispensing area, safe, receiving, and POS.
- Confidential reporting route; no retaliation for reporting.

## Failure modes (what goes wrong and the response)

| Failure | Response |
|---|---|
| Forged or altered prescription presented | Hold it, pharmacist calls the prescriber, log it, escalate to the PIC the same day. Do not fill. |
| Prescriber cannot be reached to verify | Do not fill until verified. Offer to contact the patient with an update and a firm callback time. Log the hold. |
| Claim billed for a prescription never picked up | Reverse the claim immediately (SOP-04), log the correction, and check the will-call/reversal link. |
| Suspected employee theft or diversion | Report through the confidential route the same day. PIC/owner and counsel decide the investigation. Do not confront the person or alter records. |
| Robbery or threat | People first: comply, do not resist, call police and owner/PIC when safe, preserve CCTV, log the incident, check controlled substances with the PIC. |
| Alarm or CCTV failure discovered | Log it, notify the owner/PIC the same day, and arrange repair urgently. Increase manual checks (two-person counts, locked-area checks) until it is fixed. |
| Staff member shares a login or alarm code | Revoke and reissue immediately, log the incident, and retrain. Repeat sharing triggers SOP-11 escalation. |
