## 1. The verdict this plan follows (do not relitigate it)

**CONDITIONAL GO — ACQUIRE, DO NOT BUILD. Commit no capital until five gates clear.**

Buy an existing, diligenced independent pharmacy in Brooklyn by **asset purchase, at a U.S.-multiple price (2.5–3.5× adjusted EBITDA, or 2.5–4.5× seller's discretionary earnings for a small owner-operated store), plus inventory at cost** — do not open a pharmacy from scratch as the primary route. A startup is the fallback only if no suitable target exists at a disciplined price after a genuine search, and only if the owner has a specific underserved location or captive patient flow plus capital for an 18–24-month ramp. **If any gate fails, wait — do not default to a startup.**

### The two hard facts that change everything

**SBA is CLOSED to this owner.** SBA Policy Notice 5000-876441 (effective 1 March 2026, now SOP 50 10 8.1, effective 1 October 2026) requires 100% of direct and indirect owners — and all SBA-required guarantors — to be U.S. citizens or U.S. nationals. A lawful permanent resident may not own **any** share of a 7(a)/504/Microloan borrower, **even 1%**. Older sources saying green card holders are SBA-eligible are out of date. **Never repeat the old claim that the owner is SBA-eligible.** Financing = personal capital, conventional bank loans, non-SBA CDFI loans, seller financing, equipment/inventory financing. Naturalisation would restore eligibility — that is a personal immigration decision, not a financing plan; do not plan around a future naturalisation date. Do not insert a nominal U.S.-citizen co-owner to satisfy the rule — that would be a serious compliance problem; counsel only.

**The NY Medicaid enrollment moratorium gates both routes.** CMS-approved 27 July 2026, announced 4 August 2026, it covers **new enrollments, reinstatements, and changes of ownership** for pharmacies. Pending applications are discontinued regardless of stage and do not automatically resume. Medicaid is roughly a fifth of an independent pharmacy's prescriptions — and a much larger share in the highest-ranked Brooklyn neighbourhoods — so **do not count Medicaid revenue until enrollment is active**. Whether an entity (stock/membership) purchase in which the existing enrolled entity continues escapes the moratorium is a structure-specific legal conclusion only counsel can confirm in writing.

### The economics in one paragraph

At the national average in 2024, an independent pharmacy earned **$14.57 of gross profit per prescription against a $15.00 cost to dispense it — a loss of $0.43 on the core act of dispensing**, before front-store profit and before owner pay (NCPA 2025 Digest). The business only works if the owner buys proven volume, controls inventory and reimbursement relentlessly, and earns money from services (immunisations, medication synchronisation, adherence) that are not priced by a pharmacy benefit manager. Retroactive DIR fees ended 1 January 2024 (now at point of sale), but PBMs lowered baseline rates in response — the pharmacy is paid less, sooner. **Do not forecast a per-prescription profit. Obtain the actual PBM contract rates for the store's top 50 drugs and model those.**

## 3. The role in plain English

The owner-operator is the **business owner and employer of record**, not the pharmacist. The owner runs the company; the supervising pharmacist (pharmacist-in-charge, PIC) runs the practice of pharmacy. The owner buys or builds the store, hires and pays the team, signs the lease and vendor contracts, manages cash, inventory, marketing, and the front store — and is **jointly legally responsible with the PIC** for compliance (Ed. Law §6808(2)(c), (e)), including the strength, quality, purity, and labelling of all drugs dispensed. The owner never dispenses, verifies, counsels, or overrules the PIC on a clinical decision.

**A day in the life (once trading, acquisition route):** morning — check yesterday's script count and gross profit per script against the diligence baseline, review the PBM remittance and any short-pays, confirm the controlled-substance reconciliation was done; midday — wholesaler order review with the PIC (any drug bought above its PBM MAC is a loss on every fill), prescriber visit or delivery-route check, front-store walk; afternoon — cash close (performed by one person, checked by a second, both sign), staff rota and relief cover, patient or community outreach; weekly — inventory report (value, turns, dead stock, shrinkage, top 10 variances), CPA management pack, compliance-calendar check. The PIC handles every prescription, every clinical decision, every controlled-substance order, and every patient counselling conversation. The owner's job is to make sure the PIC *can* do that safely, profitably, and without interruption.

**What "exceptional" looks like vs. "average" vs. "rejected" (hiring-manager lens, adapted to an owner):**

| | Exceptional owner-operator | Average | Fails the role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reimbursement | Knows the paid rate on the top 50 drugs before filling; appeals below-cost MACs in batches with invoices; reconciles every remittance claim-by-claim | Files claims and trusts the remittance | Discovers underpayment months later, or never |
| Inventory | Perpetual inventory reconciles to invoices; no expired drug on the shelf, ever; no high-cost stock without written authorisation + confirmed reimbursement | Counts inventory monthly; some dead stock | Stock-outs of top-50 drugs; shrinkage unexplained |
| Compliance | Audit-ready from day one: complete records, signature logs, DSCSA data retained, incident log live | Records mostly complete; fixes things when asked | Alters or back-dates a record after an audit starts |
| People | PIC secured in writing, relief pool in place, SOPs written so a successor can step in | One PIC, no cover | Opens without a pharmacist; "paper PIC" |
| Money | Working-capital reserve held intact; weekly cash position known; owner draw sized by the CPA, not by hope | Monthly cash checks | Raids working capital for build-out overruns |

The legal line is the whole map: **ownership, licensure, and authority to dispense are three separate things** (master report §2.2). Owning is not practising. The owner does not practise pharmacy by owning the store, signing the lease, or managing the front end — but **would** practise without a licence by compounding, dispensing, verifying prescriptions, or counselling patients on prescription therapy (Ed. Law §6802; Regents Rules Part 29).

### 4.3 The entity trap (must-know)

A **single-member LLC** (NY LLC Law §203; $200 filing; written Operating Agreement required; publication in 2 newspapers for 6 weeks + Certificate of Publication, $50) or a **business corporation** (BCL §402; $125) fits a non-pharmacist LPR owner. A **professional corporation (P.C.)** and a **professional service LLC (PLLC) are NOT available** to a non-pharmacist owner (BCL §1503; LLC Law §1203) — a P.C./PLLC *is* the vehicle for rendering the professional service, so its owners must be licensees. A regular LLC/corporation that *owns the registered establishment* is not itself practising pharmacy. This is the single most likely point of confusion with general business counsel — **confirm the entity choice with a NY pharmacy attorney before filing**, because the wrong entity will be rejected and cost months. Sole proprietorship / general partnership is legally permissible but **not recommended** (no liability shield).

**Premises minimums (FACT, 8 NYCRR §63.6(b)(2)):** the registered area must be ≥300 sq ft contiguous, with a compounding-dispensing area ≥100 sq ft — verified at the NYSED pre-opening inspection.

### 4.4 Roles the owner must not combine (write these into every job description)

- Owner (if not a pharmacist) must not: counsel patients, verify prescriptions, receive oral prescriptions, or supervise pharmacy practice.
- Bookkeeper must not: dispense, handle controlled-substance ordering/receipt alone, or reconcile the controlled-substance perpetual inventory.
- Delivery driver must not: counsel, take new prescription orders by phone, or decide substitutions.
- Front-store staff must be trained to say, verbatim, **"Let me get the pharmacist for you"** — and to do it.
- RPhTs (Registered Pharmacy Technicians, licensed in NY since 25 April 2021; PTCB or NHA certification) may only assist under a pharmacist's **direct personal supervision** on tasks requiring **no professional judgement**; they may not interpret a prescription, receive oral prescriptions, or counsel patients. Roster no more than **2 RPhTs per pharmacist on duty** until the current 8 NYCRR §63.14 / §29.7 ratio is confirmed in writing (master report §7.3 — the ratio text changed with Chapter 543 of the Laws of 2025, effective 29 April 2026).

## 7. Project portfolio — one hands-on project per core capability

Each project follows the job-mastery format: **objective, scenario, inputs (from the master report/model), steps, deliverable, acceptance criteria, common mistakes**. Do them in order; Projects 1–3 before any LOI, Project 4 before closing, Projects 5–6 in the first 90 days. Project 7 runs in parallel with the financing work. **Time budget: 4–8 hours per project, spread over 1–2 weeks each — a real person with limited hours can do these; none requires a pharmacy licence, because none involves dispensing.**

### Project 1 — Acquisition due-diligence pack (Capability C8, C2)

- **Objective:** produce a diligence pack on one real or practice target that would let the CPA and attorney price it — or walk away on a quantified reason.
- **Scenario:** a Brooklyn independent pharmacy is offered for sale. The broker's summary claims stable volume and "clean" audits. Verify, don't trust.
- **Inputs:** master report §9.4 checklist A–I; §9.3 valuation ranges; §9.7(b) target screen; the target's tax returns (3 years), P&L, balance sheet, monthly management accounts, bank statements (request via the attorney/CPA under NDA).
- **Steps:** (1) Pull monthly script counts for 3 years **from the dispensing system**, not the broker summary; chart the trend and name the month any decline started and what happened that month. (2) Build gross profit per script **by payer**; flag any material contract that is loss-making. (3) Reconcile DIR fees: amounts, timing, accruals, outstanding reconciliations; restate EBITDA after proper DIR accrual. (4) List the top 10 prescribers and top facilities by volume and % of scripts; flag any single-prescriber/facility concentration. (5) Collect the PBM audit history (all payers, all years available): pending/past audits, recoupment demands, amounts, appeals status. (6) Check the **address-level** PBM history, not only the seller entity's. (7) Review the lease: remaining term, renewal options and who controls them, assignment/change-of-control clause, landlord consent process. (8) Confirm the supervising pharmacist's and key staff's intentions to stay. (9) Normalise earnings (add back owner compensation above a market-rate pharmacist-manager salary, personal expenses, one-time costs; subtract costs a new owner would incur) and apply the U.S. multiples.
- **Deliverable:** a diligence pack (max 10 pages + appendices): script-trend chart, GP/script-by-payer table, normalised-earnings worksheet with every add-back documented, red-flag list with ★ items marked, and a one-paragraph walk-away or proceed recommendation with a price.
- **Acceptance criteria:** (a) script counts trace to the dispensing system, not a broker summary; (b) every add-back has a document or it does not count; (c) the price uses **2.5–3.5× adjusted EBITDA or 2.5–4.5× SDE + inventory at cost** — the Canadian 4–6× figure appears nowhere except as a rejected comparison; (d) at least one ★ item is explicitly passed or failed with evidence; (e) the CPA has reviewed the normalisation and the attorney the lease/audit sections.
- **Common mistakes:** pricing on revenue instead of GP/script by payer; accepting EBITDA before DIR accrual; ignoring the address's audit history; treating a broker's "clean audit" claim as evidence.

### Project 2 — PBM contract & reimbursement review (Capability C3)

- **Objective:** read one material PBM/PSAO agreement end-to-end with the attorney and produce a one-page contract card the owner can use at the counter.
- **Scenario:** the target (or, pre-target, a PSAO's standard agreement) offers network participation. The owner must know the rate before filling.
- **Inputs:** master report §8.3; the actual agreement; the target's top-50 drugs by volume (from the dispensing system); the wholesaler's acquisition costs for those drugs; CMS NADAC file.
- **Steps:** (1) Extract the reimbursement methodology (ingredient cost basis — AWP minus %, MAC, NADAC-based, or cost-plus — plus dispensing fee, if any). (2) Extract the MAC list: update frequency, appeal process, deadline, evidence required. (3) Extract GER/BER terms: target, reconciliation period, cap (if any) — and note explicitly if there is no cap. (4) Extract audit/recoupment rights: notice period, look-back period, extrapolation method, appeal process. (5) Extract termination and rate-change rights. (6) For the top 50 drugs, compare the PBM's MAC/rate against the wholesaler's acquisition cost; flag every drug reimbursed below acquisition cost + the cost to dispense ($15.00 national average, 2024 — use the store's own cost-to-dispense once the CPA has computed it). (7) Confirm in writing whether any 2023-or-earlier DIR reconciliation is still outstanding for the predecessor (acquisition only).
- **Deliverable:** a one-page contract card per material payer: methodology, MAC appeal route, GER/BER terms, audit rights, termination rights, and the count of top-50 drugs below cost — plus a written list of questions for the attorney.
- **Acceptance criteria:** (a) the card is built from the agreement's text, not a summary; (b) at least one below-cost drug is identified or the absence is evidenced by the rate-vs-cost comparison; (c) the attorney has confirmed the GER/BER and extrapolation readings; (d) the owner can state, without notes, what happens if the PBM changes a rate.
- **Common mistakes:** signing without reading; assuming "DIR is over" means no retroactive adjustment can happen (GER/BER survives the 2024 rule); forecasting a benefit from PBM reform or a cost-plus offer that has not been made in writing.

### Project 3 — Inventory & purchasing system (Capability C4)

- **Objective:** design the inventory system the PIC will run, using the startup model's inventory lines as the budget and the target's (or a practice store's) top-200 list as the test data.
- **Scenario:** opening inventory of **$120,000 (A) / $200,000 (B) / $300,000 (C)** (ASSUMPTION, anchored: Health Mart FDD from $60K; Crestmont 2026 $150K–$300K; ClimbTheLadder $150K–$250K) must be bought, stocked, and controlled without stranding cash in dead stock.
- **Inputs:** master report §§5.2, 7.6; the financial model's inventory and working-capital lines; wholesaler proposals (Cardinal Health, McKesson, Cencora/AmerisourceBergen — obtain competing proposals); the PMS shortlist (§7.8 quote checklist).
- **Steps:** (1) Set par levels per drug from days' supply on hand; list the top 200 drugs by volume and dollar value. (2) For each high-volume generic, compare the wholesaler's price against the PBM's MAC **before** stocking in depth. (3) Write the receiving procedure: every delivery checked the same day against invoice and purchase order (drug, strength, quantity, lot, expiry, DSCSA transaction information/statement); the person who orders must not be the only person who receives. (4) Write the FEFO, expiry-check, and dead-stock rules (any drug with no dispense in 90 days is "dead" for review — ASSUMPTION; set the threshold with the PIC). (5) Write the controlled-substance procedure: ordered only by the PIC or a pharmacist holding the PIC's DEA power of attorney, CSOS ordering, receipt and perpetual inventory reconciled the same day. (6) Write the cold-chain procedure (dedicated monitored refrigerator, calibrated thermometer/data logger, alarm, daily temperature log, quarantine on excursion). (7) Design the monthly one-page inventory report (value, turns, dead stock, shrinkage, top 10 variances).
- **Deliverable:** a written inventory SOP (max 6 pages) + a par-level sheet for the top 50 drugs + the monthly report template.
- **Acceptance criteria:** (a) every procedure names who does it and who checks it (segregation of duties); (b) no high-cost drug is stocked without a written authorisation + confirmed reimbursement rule; (c) the PIC has reviewed and signed the SOP; (d) the DSCSA solution (electronic TI/TS receipt, 6-year retention, suspect-product verification) is specified, not assumed.
- **Common mistakes:** buying the opening inventory on a wholesaler's planogram instead of the prescribers' actual prescribing; funding inventory with a credit card or short-term loan without the CPA's advice; treating the working-capital reserve as a contingency to raid for build-out overruns.

### Project 4 — Cash-flow & break-even model for the target (Capability C2, F1)

- **Objective:** rebuild the financial model on the **target's actuals** (acquisition) — or on one startup scenario (if no target yet) — and find the break-even volume, the cash trough, and the month the reserve runs out if the ramp slips.
- **Scenario:** the startup model says Scenario B breaks even at **111 Rx/day**, turns monthly EBITDA-positive in **month 8**, and is −$56,315 in Year 1. A target's actuals will differ. Prove it with numbers.
- **Inputs:** financial model (Inputs, Monthly_B, Projections_3Y, BreakEven_Sensitivity sheets); the target's receivables ageing by payer, PBM payment cycles, wholesaler terms, and actual opex (via the CPA); master report §§5.4–5.6.
- **Steps:** (1) Copy the model and replace the startup inputs with the target's actuals (volume, payer mix, margin by payer, opex, rent). (2) Compute break-even Rx/day with the exact formula: opex ÷ (26 × RevPerRx × (margin + front-end % × 45%)). (3) Build the Year-1 monthly cash flow: revenue, gross profit, opex, debt service (illustrative 60% loan at 9.5%/10 yr costs $6,141/month in Scenario B — from §5.4; use the actual term sheet when it exists), and the PBM reimbursement lag. (4) Stress it: margin at 18.2% (verified 2024 low), volume growth at +5%, a 3-month ramp slip (each month of delay below 100 Rx/day costs ~$12K–$19K of EBITDA in Scenario B). (5) Size the working-capital reserve from the result — do not reduce it until a facility is committed in writing. (6) Show the owner-draw question explicitly: the model assumes $0 draw; add the owner's required monthly draw and re-run break-even.
- **Deliverable:** a one-page model summary (break-even, first positive month, cash trough, reserve size, owner-draw sensitivity) + the rebuilt spreadsheet, with every changed input labelled and sourced.
- **Acceptance criteria:** (a) break-even reproduces from the stated formula (spot-check: B base must give 111 Rx/day); (b) the conservative variant is shown beside the base case, never hidden; (c) the cash trough month and the reserve-exhaustion month are both stated; (d) the CPA has reviewed the model; (e) no figure is presented as a forecast for a store whose contract rates have not been obtained.
- **Common mistakes:** applying the startup projections to a target; quoting the base-case 5-year ROI (~105%/172%/135%) without the conservative case; modelling 20.9% margin as a floor (the 10-year series runs 18.2%–23.3%).

### Project 5 — Compliance & controlled-substance audit (Capability C7)

- **Objective:** run a full mock audit of the store (or, pre-closing, of the target's records the seller makes available) and produce the compliance calendar that will run from day one.
- **Scenario:** a PBM desk audit arrives in the first year — the master report's evidence says audits are "no longer episodic; they are constant" and have become "a primary revenue-recovery tool for PBMs". OMIG/MFCU is the auditor for NYRx claims, with more serious consequences.
- **Inputs:** master report §§3.2, 7.7, 8.3; the audit-type table (§8.3); the 26-row licensing checklist; DEA 21 CFR Part 1304 (biennial inventory; records ≥2 years).
- **Steps:** (1) Build the compliance calendar: NYSED renewal (triennial, $260), pharmacist/RPhT renewals, DEA renewal, NPI/NCPDP maintenance, NYRx/eMedNY maintenance, PBM re-credentialing, DSCSA checks, PMP enrolment, immunisation-certification renewal, insurance renewals. (2) Sample 20 claims and test them the way a PBM would: prescription, signature log, refill documentation, prescriber communication, days' supply, quantity — count how many would be recouped in full for a missing record. (3) Reconcile the controlled-substance perpetual inventory for one week, daily; investigate every variance the same week. (4) Test the DSCSA file: electronic transaction information/statements retained, suspect-product procedure written, saleable-return verification available. (5) Test the cash close: two-person sign-off, over/short threshold (e.g. $10 — ASSUMPTION, set with the CPA), deposit matched to the close. (6) Write the audit-response procedure: PIC + owner notified the same day; attorney (and PSAO audit support) notified the same day; **no records altered, back-dated, or created after the fact**; records produced within the deadline with a cover letter and index; every recoupment appealed in writing within the deadline.
- **Deliverable:** the compliance calendar (one page) + a mock-audit report (sample results, variance log, fixes) + the written audit-response procedure.
- **Acceptance criteria:** (a) the calendar has a named owner and a date for every item; (b) the mock audit finds at least one documentation gap and the fix is written into an SOP (a mock audit that finds nothing was not run properly); (c) the PIC has reviewed the controlled-substance reconciliation; (d) the procedure states explicitly: **do not respond to OMIG/MFCU without counsel**.
- **Common mistakes:** treating a desk audit as low-stakes; "fixing" a record after an audit starts; missing an appeal deadline; waiving a copay or processing a discount card as a cash sale.

### Project 6 — Neighbourhood & patient-base analysis (Capability C6)

- **Objective:** turn the borough ranking into a block-level decision for one corridor in the top-3 shortlist — or prove that the ranking alone cannot decide it.
- **Scenario:** the master report ranks **East New York–Starrett City 78.5, East Flatbush 75.0, Brownsville–Ocean Hill 74.5** (none above 80; gaps of 3.5 points are within the proxy-cap margin). Treat them as a shortlist of three, not a single winner.
- **Inputs:** master report §4 (scoring model, weights, ranked table, evidence per neighbourhood, gaps G1–G12); NYU Furman Center profiles; LoopNet/Cushman & Wakefield listings; CMS State/County Penetration files (MA 57.77% borough-wide; Healthfirst 36.1% of MA members).
- **Steps:** (1) Pick one corridor in each shortlist neighbourhood. (2) Do a **block-level pharmacy census**: count every operating pharmacy (chain vs. independent) within 0.5 miles of a candidate site — this fills gap G1, which no published source provides. (3) Map prescribers: FQHCs, clinics, and physician offices within 0.5 miles (gap G2). (4) Get **live rent quotes** for 1,200–2,000 sq ft retail on each corridor (gap G5) and test them against the model rents ($55/$65/$70 per sq ft/yr — a prime-corridor site breaks the model). (5) Verify payer participation: Healthfirst, Aetna, UnitedHealthcare MA and NY Medicaid network terms for a new independent (master report §4.4). (6) Note the languages spoken on the corridor (Spanish, Haitian Creole, Yiddish, Chinese, Russian, Arabic are Brooklyn's major community languages — the independent's edge is delivery radius, language capacity, and prescriber relationships). (7) Score each corridor against the §9.7(b) screen, not the borough ranking.
- **Deliverable:** a one-page corridor sheet per neighbourhood (pharmacy count, prescriber map, live rent quote, payer-verification status, language/transport notes) + a ranked recommendation with the evidence and the remaining gaps stated.
- **Acceptance criteria:** (a) the pharmacy count is from a walk/drive of the corridor, not a directory (directory counts spread 982–1,423 and are not comparable); (b) at least one live rent quote per corridor; (c) the payer-verification status is "confirmed in writing", "in progress", or "not started" — never assumed; (d) the recommendation names which gaps (G1–G12) still block a lease decision.
- **Common mistakes:** choosing a neighbourhood from the ranking alone; averaging figures from different geographies (Furman ≠ PUMAs ≠ Community Districts); treating a Rite Aid closure as proof a block is underserved (it is evidence of *potential* headroom, not proof).

### Project 7 — Financing stack without SBA (Capability F1)

- **Objective:** assemble a real financing stack for one scenario (or one target) from written lender responses — no assumed terms.
- **Scenario:** Scenario B needs **$791,010** (startup) or a median-sized acquisition needs roughly **$650,000–$1.1M** to close and trade. SBA is closed. The stack must be a stack, not a single loan.
- **Inputs:** master report §§6.5, 6.11; the 12 owner inputs (§8); NYC Funds Finder; Accompany Capital (microloan $1K–$50K at 8% fixed; small-business loan $50K–$75K; NYC Future Fund $25K–$150K at 7.5% — **note: Future Fund requires ≥12 months in business, so a true startup does not qualify**); Pursuit SmartLoan to $100,000 at 11.9% fixed, 6-year; ESD Main Street Capital Loan Fund via Pursuit (up to $100,000 at 9.90% fixed, up to 6 years, interest-only first year, startups <4 years); conventional bank terms (20–30% down for an acquisition; 30%+ equity and/or substantial collateral for a startup — ASSUMPTION, bank policies vary).
- **Steps:** (1) Complete §8 first — no lender can size a stack without capital and credit band. (2) Take the free SBS / NYC Funds Finder session. (3) Approach Accompany Capital + Pursuit (own-funds products — **confirm in writing that the product is not SBA-guaranteed**) in parallel with 2 conventional banks. (4) Apply for the ESD Main Street Capital Loan via Pursuit. (5) Negotiate wholesaler terms only after the lease is signed. (6) Document the source of funds (savings, family loan, home equity) — lenders and the NYSED good-moral-character/ownership disclosures will ask. (7) Re-check the SBA SOP at the point of application — if the citizenship rule has changed or the owner has naturalised, re-price the stack with a 7(a) loan (10% equity, 10-year term) as the primary facility.
- **Deliverable:** a sources-and-uses table with a term sheet (or a written declination with reasons) for every tranche, and the equity injection stated as a dollar figure the owner can actually fund.
- **Acceptance criteria:** (a) every tranche has a written lender response; (b) personal/family capital is stated as a % of the total (the illustrative stacks assume 40% — A $175K, B $315K, C $525K — but the real figure comes from §8, Input #1); (c) no tranche is labelled a grant — every programme that provides money here is repayable debt; (d) the working-capital line is sized to several weeks of post-closing reimbursement delay.
- **Common mistakes:** budgeting a grant that does not exist (there is no standing grant programme that funds a new Brooklyn pharmacy; START-UP NY closed 1 January 2026); describing a loan as a grant; reducing the working-capital reserve because a CDFI tranche arrived.

## 10. Red lines — things that end the deal or the day

- **No LOI, no deposit, no closing date** until the attorney confirms the moratorium position in writing.
- **No SBA application** on the assumption the owner is eligible. (Re-check the SOP at the point of application; if the rule has changed or the owner has naturalised, re-price — do not assume.)
- **No P.C. or PLLC** with a non-pharmacist owner. **No nominal citizen co-owner** to game the SBA rule.
- **No opening without a pharmacist.** No "paper PIC". No PIC supervising two pharmacies. PIC change reported within **7 days**.
- **No signing a PBM agreement the owner has not read**, or one that permits unilateral rate changes without notice and without a termination right.
- **No high-cost drug stocked** for a single prescription without confirming the payer's authorisation and the reimbursement in writing first.
- **No altering, back-dating, or creating records after an audit starts.** No response to OMIG/MFCU without counsel.
- **No "fix it with a lower price"** unless the discount fully reflects a quantified exposure — in writing from the accountant.
- **No quoting the base-case ROI without the conservative case beside it.** No presenting illustrative net-profit figures as forecasts.
- **No invented retention rate.** No pharmacy-specific retention percentage exists in the sources reviewed; do not underwrite a purchase on one.
