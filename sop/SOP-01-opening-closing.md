## Purpose

Open and close the pharmacy safely, legally, and consistently every trading day, so that no patient is served, no drug is handled, and no cash is moved except while a licensed pharmacist is on duty and the premises, stock, and records are secure.

## Scope

Applies to every opening and closing of the registered premises, including early openings, late closings, holiday hours, and any day the PIC is absent. Covers the registered pharmacy area (≥300 sq ft contiguous; compounding/dispensing area ≥100 sq ft — 8 NYCRR §63.6(b)(2)), the front store, the safe/cabinet, alarms, and the cash drawer. Does not cover dispensing itself (SOP-02), controlled substances (SOP-10), or cash reconciliation detail (SOP-05).

## Owner role (who is accountable)

- **Owner (accountable):** sets opening hours, holds alarm codes and safe combinations policy, ensures a licensed pharmacist is rostered for every open hour, and reviews the opening/closing log weekly. The owner does NOT open the pharmacy area, handle prescription stock, or serve patients in the pharmacy area in the pharmacist's absence.
- **Supervising pharmacist / PIC (responsible for the pharmacy):** confirms pharmacist cover, performs or directly supervises the pharmacy-area opening/closing checks, and signs the daily log. Both owner and PIC are jointly responsible for compliance (Ed. Law §6808(2)(c)).
- **Front-store staff:** may open/close the front store only under this SOP and only when a pharmacist is present for the pharmacy area; they never enter the locked pharmacy area unsupervised.

## Prerequisites

1. NYSED pharmacy registration certificate is issued for this address and displayed; the owner's name is displayed on the exterior (Ed. Law §6808(2)(c)). Registration is address-specific.
2. A named PIC is on the registration, is NY-licensed and in good standing, and is rostered for the day. A relief pharmacist is on call.
3. Alarm codes, keys, and safe combinations are issued per person, never shared, and recorded in the access register (SOP-09).
4. Opening hours are posted. **[CONFIRM WITH COUNSEL/CPA]:** confirm the exact posted-hours and signage requirements for this premises with counsel before first use.
5. The daily opening/closing checklist (paper or system form) is available at the pharmacy entrance.

## Numbered steps

### Opening

1. Arrive at the posted opening time. The designated opener checks the exterior for signs of forced entry, damage, or alarm activation before unlocking. If anything is wrong, do NOT enter alone — call the PIC and the owner, and follow SOP-09 (incident reporting).
2. Disarm the alarm with your own code. Record the time and your name on the opening log.
3. Confirm the pharmacist on duty is present in the pharmacy area before unlocking the pharmacy area. If no licensed pharmacist is present, keep the pharmacy area locked and do NOT dispense, sell prescription drugs, or accept new prescriptions. Front-store-only trading may proceed only if counsel has confirmed that arrangement for this premises — **[CONFIRM WITH COUNSEL/CPA]**.
4. The pharmacist on duty unlocks the pharmacy area, the controlled-substance safe/cabinet (SOP-10), and the dispensing workstations. Front-store staff unlock only the front store.
5. The pharmacist checks: refrigerator temperature (log it — see SOP-03), controlled-substance safe seal/count status, will-call and delivery bins, and that no expired stock is on the shelf from the prior day.
6. The pharmacist verifies the pharmacy management system, e-prescribing (Surescripts), PBM switch, and PMP/I-STOP access are live. Report any outage to the PIC the same morning; do not dispense controlled substances until PMP access is confirmed working (SOP-10).
7. The cashier counts the opening cash float against the float record, signs the drawer count, and reports any variance to the PIC/owner before the first sale (SOP-05).
8. The PIC (or pharmacist on duty) signs the opening log, confirming pharmacist presence, fridge temperature, and system status. Only then is the pharmacy open for dispensing.

### Closing

9. Stop accepting new prescriptions at the posted cut-off time set by the PIC. Complete all in-progress fills and counsel patients before closing; do not rush a final verification.
10. The pharmacist secures all filled-but-uncollected prescriptions in will-call, logs unclaimed items per SOP-02, and returns any undelivered items to the pharmacy the same day (SOP-06).
11. The pharmacist locks the controlled-substance safe/cabinet, checks the perpetual controlled-substance count against the day's dispensing (SOP-10), and records any variance immediately.
12. The cashier performs the daily close with a second person checking it (SOP-05): reconcile POS total to system sales and physical cash, reconcile card totals to processor settlement, and deposit cash per the fixed schedule.
13. The pharmacist checks the refrigerator temperature a final time, logs it, and confirms cold-chain products are stored correctly. Quarantine any product exposed to a temperature excursion and report it to the PIC (SOP-03).
14. Front-store staff tidy and lock the front store; the pharmacist locks the pharmacy area separately. The registered area must be physically secured so drugs are inaccessible when no pharmacist is on duty (Ed. Law §6808(2)(f)).
15. The last person out sets the alarm with their own code, locks the exterior, and signs the closing log with the time. Record any incident, variance, or alarm event on the same log.

## Controls (what prevents error)

- Two-person rule at close: one person performs the cash close, a second checks and both sign.
- Pharmacy area cannot be unlocked without a licensed pharmacist present; the log requires the pharmacist's signature.
- Per-person alarm codes and keys — never shared — so every entry is attributable.
- Refrigerator temperature logged twice daily; an excursion triggers quarantine, not silent use.
- Opening is blocked (pharmacy area stays locked) if the PIC/relief pharmacist is absent — the SOP defaults to closed, not to trading without a pharmacist.

## Failure modes (what goes wrong and the response)

| Failure | Response |
|---|---|
| PIC calls in sick; no relief pharmacist available | Do NOT open the pharmacy area. Front store only if counsel has confirmed that is lawful for this premises; otherwise close the whole store. Notify patients with promised pickups by phone. Log the closure and the reason. |
| Alarm activated overnight / signs of break-in | Do not enter alone. Call PIC and owner, then police if warranted. Photograph, log, and follow SOP-09. Do not touch stock until the PIC checks the controlled-substance inventory (SOP-10). |
| Refrigerator out of range at opening | Quarantine affected products, log the excursion, notify the PIC immediately. Do not dispense quarantined stock. Follow SOP-03 cold-chain steps. |
| Cash float does not match at opening | Stop, recount with a second person, log the variance, notify owner/PIC before trading. Do not "make it up" from sales. See SOP-05. |
| Systems (PMS/PBM/PMP) down at opening | PIC decides whether safe dispensing can proceed; controlled substances are not dispensed until PMP access is restored. Log the outage and the PIC's decision. |
| PIC changes | Notify NYSED within 7 days (Form PH-205, Regents Rule 29.7(a)(10)) and apply for an amended registration. Do not operate without a named PIC. |
