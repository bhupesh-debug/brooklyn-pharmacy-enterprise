## Purpose

Keep the right drugs, in the right quantities, in date, from authorised sources only — and prove it with a perpetual inventory that reconciles to invoices, so that stock-outs, expiry, dead stock, and shrinkage are caught early and no drug is dispensed that should not be.

## Scope

All prescription and front-store stock: ordering, receiving, storage (including cold chain), perpetual inventory, cycle counts, full counts, expiry management, dead stock, returns/recalls, and DSCSA transaction data. Controlled substances are ordered/received under SOP-10 as well; this SOP covers the general inventory rules that also apply to them.

## Owner role (who is accountable)

- **Owner (accountable):** approves the wholesaler agreement (with attorney/CPA review of payment terms, security interest/UCC filing, and any personal guarantee), sets inventory budgets and par-level policy with the PIC, and reviews the monthly one-page inventory report.
- **PIC (responsible):** owns stocking decisions, generic-vs-MAC comparisons, expiry and recall actions, and all inventory counts. The PIC decides dead-stock disposition.
- **Bookkeeper:** posts receipts/payables, produces the monthly inventory report, and counts with the PIC — never counts or reconciles controlled substances alone (SOP-10).
- **RPhT / front-store staff:** receive, shelve, rotate, and count only as directed by the pharmacist; never order controlled substances and never approve a write-off.

## Numbered steps

### Ordering

1. Order only from an authorised trading partner (licensed wholesaler/manufacturer) with full DSCSA transaction data. Never buy from a grey-market, internet, or "too cheap to be true" source — refuse and report the offer to the PIC.
2. The PIC (or pharmacist directed by the PIC) reviews the order list against par levels, days' supply on hand, and the top-200 drugs by volume and dollar value (reviewed monthly).
3. Before stocking a new generic in depth, compare the wholesaler's generic price against the PBM's MAC. A generic bought above its MAC loses money on every fill — do not stock it in depth. Record the comparison weekly.
4. Do not stock a high-cost brand/specialty drug for a single prescription without confirming the payer's authorisation and the reimbursement in writing first (SOP-04). A single specialty fill can strand tens of thousands of dollars.
5. Place the order through the PMS/wholesaler portal. Keep a second wholesaler active for stock-outs and price comparison on high-volume generics.
6. Controlled substances are ordered only by the PIC or a pharmacist holding the PIC's DEA power of attorney, on the DEA-approved ordering system (CSOS). See SOP-10.

### Receiving

7. Check every delivery the same day against invoice and purchase order: drug, strength, quantity, lot, expiry, and DSCSA electronic TI/TS. Record any discrepancy on the receiving log the same day.
8. The person who orders a drug must NOT be the only person who receives and checks it. A second person checks high-value and controlled-substance receipts.
9. Check expiry on every receipt. Flag short-dated stock (expiring within 90 days — **assumption** in the master report; set the threshold with the PIC) and use the wholesaler's return/exchange policy before expiry.
10. Shelve by FEFO: the earliest-expiring unit is dispensed first. Rotate on every receipt and on the monthly shelf check.
11. Store refrigerated products only in the dedicated pharmacy refrigerator. Log the temperature at least daily (twice daily per SOP-01). Any excursion: quarantine the product, notify the PIC immediately, and do not dispense it until the PIC clears it.

### Counts and reconciliation

12. Maintain a perpetual inventory in the PMS: every dispense, receipt, return, transfer, waste/destruction, and adjustment moves the perpetual count the same day.
13. Cycle-count high-value and high-theft items weekly. Count the full inventory monthly in the early phases (PIC + bookkeeper together).
14. Reconcile the perpetual count to wholesaler invoices and DSCSA data. Investigate every variance before it is written off; document the explanation. No variance is written off by the person who caused or counted it alone.
15. Controlled substances: reconcile the perpetual inventory the same day as receipt and dispensing, per SOP-10. Any controlled-substance variance is investigated the same week.

### Expiry, dead stock, returns, and recalls

16. Check expiry monthly on the shelf. No expired drug stays on the shelf, ever. Remove, quarantine, and log expired stock the day it is found.
17. Review dead stock monthly: any drug with no dispense in 90 days is "dead" for review (**assumption** in the master report; confirm the threshold with the PIC). The PIC decides: return for credit, transfer (only where lawful and documented), or write-off.
18. Returns: follow the written returns procedure; record drug, quantity, lot, reason, and credit received. Never return a drug to a source that is not an authorised trading partner.
19. Recalls: action every recall the same day. Quarantine affected lots immediately, document the action, and notify the PIC. Do not dispense quarantined stock under any circumstances.

### Reporting

20. The bookkeeper produces a monthly one-page inventory report to the owner: inventory value, inventory turns, days' supply on hand, dead-stock value, shrinkage, and top 10 variances. The PIC reviews and signs it. **[CONFIRM WITH COUNSEL/CPA]:** no verified Brooklyn benchmark for turns/shrinkage was in the master report — the CPA must set targets from the store's own first-quarter data and the wholesaler's terms; do not invent a target.

## Controls (what prevents error)

- Authorised trading partners only, with DSCSA TI/TS on every receipt.
- Segregation of duties: ordering ≠ sole receiving; dispensing ≠ self-verification; bookkeeper posting ≠ approving.
- FEFO rotation and expiry checks on receipt and monthly.
- Perpetual inventory with weekly cycle counts and monthly full counts (PIC + bookkeeper together).
- Generic-vs-MAC check before depth stocking; written authorisation + reimbursement confirmation before high-cost stocking.
- Cold-chain monitoring with alarm, daily logging, and quarantine on excursion.

## Failure modes (what goes wrong and the response)

| Failure | Response |
|---|---|
| Stock-out of a top-50 drug during opening hours | Same-day wholesaler order, patient callback with a firm time, and a PIC review of the par level. Log the stock-out. |
| Expired drug found on the shelf | Remove and quarantine immediately, log it, notify the PIC, and check whether any expired unit was dispensed (if so, follow SOP-02 error handling). |
| Perpetual count does not match the shelf count | Investigate before write-off. Check receiving, dispensing, returns, and transfers. Escalate unexplained variances to the PIC; controlled-substance variances follow SOP-10 the same week. |
| Wholesaler delivery short / wrong drug / damaged | Log the discrepancy the same day, contact the wholesaler, do not dispense the wrong/damaged product, and keep the invoice discrepancy open until credited. |
| Temperature excursion | Quarantine, log, notify PIC. Do not dispense affected products until the PIC clears them in writing. |
| Recall notice received | Quarantine affected lots the same day, document, notify PIC, and follow the recall instructions. Check will-call and recent dispensing records if the PIC directs it. |
| Suspect / illegitimate product | Quarantine immediately, notify the PIC, and make the FDA/trading-partner notification within the required timeframe — **[CONFIRM WITH COUNSEL/CPA]:** verify the current FDA timeframe (24 hours of confirmation is stated in secondary sources only). |
