## Purpose

Make sure every patient who needs a pharmacist's advice gets it from a pharmacist, documented — and that every immunisation is authorised, screened, given safely, observed, reported, and billed to the correct benefit.

## Scope

Patient counselling (offer, counselling, refusal, documentation, referrals), medication reconciliation and medication therapy management (MTM) where offered, and immunisations (authority, screening, consent, administration, observation, anaphylaxis readiness, documentation, registry reporting, billing). Covers only the immunisations NY authorises a certified pharmacist to give (see step 10). Does not cover dispensing (SOP-02) or delivery (SOP-06).

## Owner role (who is accountable)

- **Owner (accountable):** funds the private immunisation area, ensures a certified immuniser is rostered for every immunisation session, and never directs a clinical decision or counsels a patient. The owner approves immunisation billing policy with the PIC/CPA/attorney.
- **PIC (responsible):** owns the counselling and immunisation programmes, holds (or ensures a staff pharmacist holds) the immunisation certification, owns the standing order, and reviews counselling and immunisation records.
- **Staff pharmacist/intern:** counsel, reconcile medications, and administer immunisations only within their certification and the standing order.
- **RPhT / front-store staff:** hand over completed prescriptions while advising the patient that pharmacist counselling is available; book immunisation appointments; never counsel.

## Prerequisites

1. A private area for immunisation that provides for the patient's privacy is available (Ed. Law §6801).
2. The immunising pharmacist holds NYSED immunisation certification, an approved immunisation course within the past 3 years, and current CPR/BLS.
3. A non-patient-specific (standing) order signed by a NY-licensed physician or nurse practitioner is in place — or a patient-specific order for the patient. **[CONFIRM WITH COUNSEL/CPA]:** the precise scope of the non-patient-specific order, the record-keeping for it, and whether any additional NYC requirement applies — confirm with the attorney before the first clinic (master report, §8.2.6).
4. Epinephrine is on hand, in date, and staff are trained in anaphylaxis response. A 15-minute observation area/process is set.
5. Registry reporting is set up: Citywide Immunization Registry (CIR) for NYC (Brooklyn is in NYC). NYSIIS applies outside NYC.
6. Billing routes are confirmed with the PIC/CPA before the first vaccine is given: Medicare Part B roster billing (mass immuniser with PTAN) vs Part D vs commercial/medical benefit vs cash — per patient, per vaccine, know which benefit to bill before the vaccine is given. **[CONFIRM WITH COUNSEL/CPA]:** the 2026 Part B administration fee stated in one secondary source ($34.62) is NOT verified from a CMS fee schedule — obtain the current CMS Part B fee before forecasting immunisation revenue. Pending immunisation-expansion bills (e.g. A.8383, A.4346, S.8496, 2025–2026 session) are NOT enacted — do not plan on them.

## Numbered steps

### Counselling

1. **Offer counselling on every new prescription** (and on refills where the pharmacist judges it needed). The offer is made by the pharmacist/intern, not by the cashier alone.
2. **Counsel in private where the patient prefers it.** Cover: what the drug is for, how to take it, what to do if a dose is missed, common side effects, interactions (including OTC and alcohol where relevant), storage, and when to seek help.
3. **Document the offer and the outcome:** counselling given (by whom, key points) or the patient's refusal. Federal OBRA '90 counselling standards apply to Medicaid patients; document to the PBM audit standard.
4. **Medication reconciliation.** The pharmacist reviews the patient's full medication list, identifies duplications, omissions, discrepancies, and interactions, and — with the patient's consent — contacts the prescriber(s) to resolve them. Document the review and any prescriber contact.
5. **Referrals.** Refer the patient to their prescriber or to urgent care where the pharmacist judges it needed. Document the referral. The pharmacist never diagnoses or prescribes outside NY scope of practice.
6. **MTM / adherence services (only if contracted).** Offer comprehensive medication reviews only through a contracted MTM platform and fee. Do not forecast or charge an MTM fee that has not been contracted in writing. Blister/multi-dose packaging is done under the pharmacist's supervision with a final pharmacist check, and any service fee is disclosed to the patient before the service starts (fee set with the CPA; no fee is stated in the master report).

### Immunizations

7. **Check authority before booking.** The pharmacist confirms the vaccine and the patient's age are within the authorised scope (step 10) and that a certified immuniser will be on duty.
8. **Screen the patient.** Check allergies, contraindications, prior reactions, current illness, and pregnancy status where relevant, using the screening form. If any screening answer requires judgment, the pharmacist decides — do not vaccinate on a form alone.
9. **Obtain consent.** Explain the vaccine, its benefits and risks, and obtain and record the patient's consent (and, for under-19s, the parent/guardian consent as required). Record consent in the immunisation record.
10. **Authorised scope (verified, NYSDOH/NYSED):** a certified pharmacist may administer **influenza vaccine to patients aged 2 years and older**, and **pneumococcal, meningococcal, tetanus, diphtheria, pertussis, herpes zoster (shingles), hepatitis A, hepatitis B, HPV, measles, mumps, rubella, varicella, COVID-19, and RSV** to **adults 18 years and older** — under a patient-specific order or a non-patient-specific (standing) order. Legal basis: Ed. Law §§6527, 6801, 6909; 8 NYCRR §63.9. Do not administer any vaccine or to any age group outside this scope.
11. **Administer the vaccine** in the private area, following the standing order, the vaccine's storage/handling requirements, and infection-control practice. Record vaccine, lot, expiry, site/route, dose, and administrator.
12. **Observe for 15 minutes.** Keep the patient in the observation area for 15 minutes. Watch for an adverse reaction. If anaphylaxis is suspected, follow the anaphylaxis procedure immediately (epinephrine, call emergency services, notify the PIC).
13. **Document and report.** Record the immunisation in the PMS/immunisation record the same day. Report all immunisations to persons **under 19** to the CIR; report immunisations to persons **19 and older** to the CIR **upon the patient's consent** (Public Health Law §2168).
14. **Bill the correct benefit.** Bill Medicare Part B (roster billing, vaccine + administration, HCPCS G0008/G0009/G0010) for Part B vaccines (influenza, pneumococcal, COVID-19, hepatitis B for at-risk/unknown-history patients) where the pharmacy is an enrolled mass immuniser; bill Part D through the PBM for Part D vaccines (shingles, RSV, Tdap, other ACIP-recommended adult vaccines not in Part B); bill the medical or pharmacy benefit per the patient's commercial/Medicaid coverage; or charge the posted cash price for uninsured patients. Confirm the benefit before the vaccine is given. Document the benefit billed and the amount.
15. **Follow up adverse events.** Record any adverse event reported by the patient, notify the PIC the same day, and follow counsel's advice on any reporting (including VAERS where applicable — **[CONFIRM WITH COUNSEL/CPA]:** confirm the VAERS/reporting route with the PIC/attorney before the first clinic).

## Controls (what prevents error)

- Counselling and immunisation are pharmacist-only; non-pharmacists fetch the pharmacist.
- Counselling offer documented on every new prescription; refusal documented, never invented.
- Standing order + certification + CPR/BLS checked before every immunisation session.
- Screening, consent, 15-minute observation, and epinephrine readiness on every immunisation.
- Registry reporting (CIR) and benefit-billing check before the vaccine is given.
- No vaccine or age group outside the authorised scope in step 10.

## Failure modes (what goes wrong and the response)

| Failure | Response |
|---|---|
| Patient was not offered counselling on a new prescription | Log as a near-miss, notify the PIC, and retrain the staff involved. Do not back-fill a counselling record that did not happen. |
| Patient refuses counselling | Document the refusal. Do not record counselling that did not happen. |
| Vaccine given outside authorised scope or without a valid order | Stop the clinic. Notify the PIC and the attorney the same day. Log the incident and follow counsel's advice. Do not repeat the session until the order/scope is corrected. |
| Adverse reaction / anaphylaxis | Follow the anaphylaxis procedure immediately, call emergency services, notify the PIC, document everything, and follow counsel's advice on reporting. |
| Registry report missed or late | File the report as soon as discovered, log the delay, and notify the PIC. Add a same-day reporting check to the clinic close. |
| Wrong benefit billed | Correct the billing the same day where possible, log the correction, and notify the bookkeeper/CPA. Review the benefit-check step (step 14) with the staff involved. |
| Certified immuniser is absent on a clinic day | Cancel or reschedule the clinic. Do not let an uncertified pharmacist or any non-pharmacist immunise. |
