## How this plan is built (the handson method, adapted)

This plan follows the controlling hands-on spec (`~/workspace/custom-skills/handson/SKILL.md`): learn/do by doing, not by reading theory. Adapted to daily pharmacy operations, that means:

- **At least 60% of operating effort is active practice** — filling, verifying, reconciling, counting, counselling — never replaced by reading SOPs or watching training videos.
- **Every major process follows Understand → Observe → Replicate → Create → Evaluate → Demonstrate.** The PIC explains it, shows it done once, the technician/staff member does it supervised, then does it independently, the PIC reviews the record against the professional standard, and the completed record (signed log, reconciled report, audit-ready file) is the evidence of competence.
- **Every task names who does it, how long it takes, and what "done" looks like** — a named record, signed by the person who did it and checked by a second person where the report requires segregation of duties.
- **A project ladder, not a single launch:** Stage 1 pre-opening simulation → Stage 2 ramp (months 1–6) → Stage 3 steady state (months 7–18) → Stage 4 growth (18 months+, only if volume, payer mix and margin justify it) → Stage 5 capstone: a full quarterly business review the owner can defend to the CPA and attorney. See §8.
- **A mastery scorecard and a gap register** (§8) are kept live; a score is never inflated, and a gap is never closed by completing a course — only by a verified record.
- **No owner facts are invented.** The owner's name, available capital, credit profile, and preferred Brooklyn neighbourhood were not supplied and are not assumed anywhere in this plan. Where the plan needs one, it says **OWNER INPUT REQUIRED** and points to §9.

## The verdict this plan follows (not relitigates)

**CONDITIONAL GO — ACQUIRE, DO NOT BUILD; commit no capital until five gates clear.**

1. **Medicaid moratorium resolved in writing** — see the hard fact below.
2. **A suitable target exists at a disciplined price** — stable or growing script count over three years; gross profit per script by payer verified and positive on the material contracts; no open or recently settled PBM audit with material recoupment; no single-prescriber or single-facility concentration; a lease with at least five years remaining or renewal options the buyer controls; the supervising pharmacist and key staff willing to stay; inventory verified by count; a seller willing to stay for a transition period.
3. **Supervising pharmacist secured in writing** — New York-licensed, full-time (30+ hours/week), supervising only this one pharmacy, named on the registration.
4. **Financing confirmed without SBA** — conventional bank loan and/or CDFI stack plus personal capital, with the equity injection and a working-capital line sized to several weeks of post-closing reimbursement delay.
5. **Healthcare attorney + pharmacy-savvy CPA engaged pre-LOI** — before any letter of intent, lease, wholesaler agreement, or lender application.

If any gate cannot be met for a specific target, **do not proceed with that target**. The fallback is to wait for a suitable target — **not** to default to a startup. If the owner cannot secure a full-time supervising pharmacist at all, the answer is **do neither**. This operating plan therefore describes how the pharmacy runs **once acquired (or, only if the flip condition in the master report §10.9 triggers, once built)** — it is not a green light to build, and no task in it authorises spending before the gates clear.

### 1.4 The prescription journey (the daily spine — §7.5 of the report)

1. **Intake** — e-prescription via Surescripts (pharmacist/intern reviews + initials acceptance); written Rx checked for completeness; oral Rx **pharmacist-only**; refill check (refills remaining, last fill, days' supply, authorisation, "too soon"/duplicate flags). Two patient identifiers at every handoff.
2. **Data entry + DUR** — RPhT keys data; system runs prospective DUR (drug–drug, drug–allergy, duplicate therapy, age/sex, dose range, controlled-substance history). **Pharmacist verifies all information before dispensing** — mandatory, recorded.
3. **Controlled substances** — PMP/I-STOP checked before dispensing; documented. Schedule II: no refills; strict quantity/dating/record rules.
4. **Insurance adjudication** — real-time claim via the switch; rejections worked same day where possible. No coverage / not covered → **pharmacist** (not cashier) explains cash price and prescriber-alternative option.
5. **Filling** — RPhT retrieves stock (first-expired, first-out), counts, labels, stages. **Final product verification — pharmacist-only** (drug, strength, form, quantity, label vs prescription), then release. Barcode scanning is an aid, not a substitute.
6. **Counselling** — offer of counselling on **every new prescription** (and refills where the pharmacist judges it needed); offer/counselling/refusal documented (OBRA '90 standard for Medicaid; PBM audit standard). Covers purpose, how to take it, missed dose, side effects, interactions (incl. OTC/alcohol), storage, when to seek help. Medication reconciliation at this point, with patient consent, prescriber contacted to resolve discrepancies.
7. **Will-call / pickup / delivery** — two-identifier check at pickup; copay at POS; counselling offer repeated if new; signature/proof-of-receipt log where the PBM/SOP requires it (signature logs are a leading PBM audit denial point). Delivery sealed, labelled, logged out; proof of delivery; same-day return of undelivered items.
8. **Post-dispensing** — electronic audit trail (who did what, when); errors/near-misses into the incident log same day; PIC review; SOP change.

**Throughput planning — ASSUMPTION (report §7.5):** a new store will not fill the national average of ~211 Rx/day (65,795/year, NCPA 2026 Digest) on opening. Plan the rota for a ramp (e.g. 30–60/day in month 1, rising with prescriber outreach and payer enrolment — ASSUMPTION), and roster the second pharmacist only when the queue, phone, and counselling load demonstrably exceed one pharmacist's safe capacity. **Safe capacity is the PIC's professional judgment, not a spreadsheet target.** For an acquired store, the inherited script count replaces the ramp assumption — verify it in diligence (stable/growing over three years) before rostering to it.

## 5. The operating cycle — how each layer feeds the next

```
DAILY (32 items: opening 12 · midday 8 · closing 12)
  │  Every task leaves a signed record (queue, PMP, perpetual,
  │  temperature, cash close, incident log).
  ▼
WEEKLY (14 tasks)
  │  Daily records are reconciled: cycle counts test the perpetual,
  │  PBM remittances are matched claim-by-claim, expiry and
  │  refunds are reviewed, the one-page dashboard is produced.
  │  The dashboard is the week's truth — if a daily record is
  │  missing, the weekly reconciliation finds it.
  ▼
MONTHLY (16 tasks)
  │  Weekly dashboards + reconciliations become the management
  │  pack: P&L (Rx vs front-end kept separate), full inventory
  │  count, controlled-substance reconciliation, PIC self-audit,
  │  13-week cash forecast. The pack tests the month against
  │  break-even (§7) and the working-capital reserve.
  ▼
QUARTERLY (8 tasks)
  │  Three monthly packs become the quarterly review: scenario
  │  re-tested (base vs conservative), contracts and U&C
  │  re-priced, compliance programme reviewed, scorecard updated.
  │  A quarterly decision (correct course / stop a line) is minuted.
  ▼
ANNUAL (10 tasks)
    Renewals, annual statements, tax, re-credentialing, SOP review,
    and the plan reset. The annual review sets next year's
    Rx/day, margin, and service targets — which become next
    year's daily huddle targets (O11). The cycle closes.
```

| Layer | Question it answers | If it fails, what happens |
|---|---|---|
| Daily | Was every prescription verified, every controlled drug accounted for, and every dollar reconciled **today**? | Weekly reconciliation catches it within days — a missing signature log or an unreconciled controlled count becomes a PBM/DEA exposure |
| Weekly | Are we being paid what the contracts say, is stock real, and is the dashboard honest? | Monthly pack is built on unreconciled numbers — break-even review is fiction |
| Monthly | Are we above or below break-even, is cash safe for 13 weeks, and is the inventory real? | Quarterly review has no basis; the cash trough (months 1–6 in a startup; post-closing reimbursement delay in an acquisition) arrives unannounced |
| Quarterly | Is the model still true (margin, mix, rent %), and are contracts/compliance current? | Annual plan is set on stale rates; a GER/BER clawback or an expired credentialing lands without warning |
| Annual | Are we licensed, insured, audited, and aimed at the right targets for next year? | Registration/DEA/licence lapse, uninsured exposure, or a second year run on first-year assumptions |

**The one rule that holds the cycle together:** a task with no record is treated as not done at the next layer up. The daily close (C6) is checked by a second person; the weekly PBM reconciliation (W5) is reviewed by the PIC; the monthly pack (Mth1) is signed by owner + PIC; the quarterly review (Q1) is minuted with a decision. No layer signs off on the layer below sight-unseen.

## 6. Staffing rota logic

### 6.1 Coverage rules (non-negotiable)

1. **Never open without a licensed pharmacist present.** Ed. Law §6808(2)(e): "Every pharmacy shall be under the immediate supervision and management of a licensed pharmacist at all hours when open." If the rostered pharmacist calls in sick, the store does not open until relief cover is on the premises — the front store does not trade alone, and no prescription is dispensed, verified, or handed over without a pharmacist.
2. **PIC: 30+ hours/week, one pharmacy only, named on the registration.** A part-time or "on-call" PIC does not satisfy the rule. The PIC's role must be active, not passive. PIC change: notify NYSED **within 7 days** (Form PH-205) and apply for an amended registration.
3. **RPhT ratio: max 2 RPhTs per pharmacist on duty; max 4 supervised individuals at one time** (verify current §29.7(a)(21)/§63.14 text before rostering — §0). Interns are exempt from the ratio but supervised under Part 63.
4. **Pharmacist cover for all open hours:** if the PIC works 30 hours and the store is open 58 (Mon–Fri 9–7 = 50 hrs + Sat 9–5 = 8 hrs, ASSUMPTION pattern), the remaining ~28 hours need staff/relief pharmacists. The rota is built backwards from open hours, not forwards from the PIC's availability.
5. **Immunisation sessions** are staffed only when a SED-certified immuniser (approved course within past 3 years + current CPR/BLS) is on duty, in the private area.

### 7.2 Break-even sensitivity — the table the monthly review uses

Break-even Rx/day = monthly opex ÷ (26 days × revenue per Rx × (margin + front-end % × 45%)). (Report §5.5.)

| Revenue/Rx ↓ / Margin → | 18.2% (conservative, VERIFIED 2024) | **20.9% (base, VERIFIED 2025)** | 23.0% (optimistic, ASSUMPTION) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **$75.00 (conservative)** | A 82 / B 134 / C 205 | A 72 / B 118 / C 181 | A 66 / B 108 / C 166 |
| **$79.20 (base, VERIFIED)** | A 78 / B 127 / C 194 | **A 68 / B 111 / C 171** | A 62 / B 102 / C 157 |
| **$85.00 (optimistic)** | A 73 / B 118 / C 181 | A 64 / B 104 / C 160 | A 58 / B 95 / C 146 |

**Reading it honestly:** Scenario A is fragile — at conservative margin its break-even is 73–82 Rx/day, and at $75 it is 82, **above** its steady-state target (80); in the conservative variant Lean **never breaks even**. Scenario B has the widest safety margin (base 111 vs target 150, 26% headroom; even at 18.2% it breaks even at 127). Scenario C needs the most volume (171 base) but has the most headroom vs target (220) **provided** the delivery/facility volume actually materialises — the least certain assumption in the file. **Do not chase high-ticket specialty without checking its margin and DIR exposure; do not forecast a per-prescription profit — obtain the actual PBM contract rates for the top 50 drugs and model those.**

### 7.3 The profit-engine logic (why dispensing alone cannot carry the store)

1. **Dispensing is a loss at the national average** (−$0.43/Rx). Every fill must therefore be judged on its **actual adjudicated paid amount vs acquisition cost** (M2), not on volume alone. A below-cost fill is a documented decision by the pharmacist/owner — never a default.
2. **Generics are where a positive spread is most likely** (acquisition below the PBM's MAC); brands are commonly reimbursed at or below acquisition cost plus a small fee, and some brand/specialty fills reimburse **below** acquisition cost. The MAC check (W2) is weekly, not one-off.
3. **Front-end gross profit is tracked separately from Rx gross profit** (Mth1) — combining them hides whether the pharmacy or the shop is carrying the business.
4. **Clinical services are the only revenue not priced by a PBM:** immunisations (build properly — SED-certified immuniser, private area, standing order, correct billing rail, CIR reporting), med-sync/adherence (retention + performance; build from month 1; **do not forecast a fee not contracted**), MTM (some fees; contract first). LTC is **Phase 3 only, contract first**.
5. **Cash Rx is the highest-margin prescription stream per fill when priced correctly** — the only stream where the pharmacy sets the price and the only stream with no clawback. Price deliberately (Q3); discount cards are third-party claims, not cash sales, and are never processed as cash.
6. **Medicaid (NYRx) is core volume in Brooklyn but gated:** NYRx pays on the state's FFS basis (ingredient + dispensing fee — **current NYRx fee schedule UNVERIFIED; obtain it from eMedNY/NYSDOH before forecasting Medicaid revenue**), with no PBM spread or DIR — but OMIG/MFCU audit replaces PBM audit, the documentation standard is higher, and **no Medicaid revenue is counted until enrollment is active** (moratorium, §Hard facts).

### 7.4 Daily → financial-target traceability

| Daily/weekly task | Financial line it protects |
|---|---|
| O5/M1 fill-queue discipline; C1 promised-today close | Rx/day ramp — every unfilled promised prescription is lost volume and lost trust |
| M2 adjudication spot-check; W2 MAC check | Gross profit per Rx — stops below-cost fills becoming the default |
| W5 PBM reconciliation; Mth7 audit-readiness; Mth8 rate check | Realised margin — the difference between the adjudicated price and the money actually kept after recoupments |
| W4 cycle count; Mth3 full count; Mth4 inventory report | Inventory value, turns, shrinkage — inventory is the largest use of cash and the largest source of loss if mismanaged |
| C6/C7 cash close + deposit; Mth14 13-week forecast | Cash survival — PBM receivables lag 30–60 days vs wholesaler payables 14–30 days; the working-capital reserve funds the trough |
| W9 outreach; W11 immunisation review; Q7 services review | Volume + non-PBM revenue — the two levers that actually move break-even |

### 7.5 The cash trap (read before the first month)

In a startup, months 1–6 are deeply negative (rent + payroll + inventory replenishment while PBM receivables build); inflection in months 7–10; monthly EBITDA positive from month 8 (B), 9 (C), or 10 (A) — **but Year 1 as a whole is EBITDA-negative in every scenario**. The working-capital reserve (§5.2: 4/5/6 months of opex by scenario — A $122,564, B $255,250, C $480,498) is what funds this trough; without it the pharmacy runs out of cash in months 4–6 even though the model later turns profitable. In an acquisition, the same discipline applies to the post-closing reimbursement delay (several weeks — size the working-capital line to it, Gate 4). Monthly cash flow after illustrative debt service (60% loan at 9.5%/10 yr — ILLUSTRATIVE, report §5.4) is worse by $3,379 (A) / $6,141 (B) / $10,165 (C) per month. **Hold the full reserve; treat it as spent on day one, not as a contingency to raid for build-out overruns.**

## 8. Hands-on ramp — project ladder, scorecard, gap register

Adapted from the handson spec §§6, 10, 11: five progressively difficult stages, each with an objective, a real-world scenario, deliverables, quality standards, and an evaluation rubric — applied to operating the pharmacy, not to classroom learning.

### 8.1 The five-stage operating ladder

| Stage | Project | Purpose | Deliverables (evidence) | Quality standard |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Pre-opening simulation** — run the full daily cycle (O1–O12, M1–M8, C1–C12) on paper with the PIC before opening: mock prescriptions through intake → DUR → fill → verification → counselling → will-call; mock controlled-substance receipt + perpetual reconciliation; mock cash close | Understand fundamentals; find SOP gaps before a real patient is involved | Completed mock logs, signed by PIC; SOP corrections list | Every mock prescription has a complete audit trail; every mock variance is investigated, not written off |
| 2 | **Ramp operation (months 1–6)** — operate at 30–60 Rx/day rising (ASSUMPTION ramp), one pharmacist + relief, 0–1 RPhT; weekly PBM reconciliation from week 1; first monthly pack by month 1 | Apply the cycle under real volume; survive the cash trough | 6 weekly dashboards, 6 monthly packs, first PIC self-audit | No week without a dashboard; no month without a pack; no controlled count unreconciled |
| 3 | **Steady-state operation (months 7–18)** — 1 PIC + 1 staff pharmacist, 2 RPhTs; full monthly inventory count; quarterly business review; immunisation programme running (if certified) | Solve realistic problems: PBM audit letter, MAC appeal batch, stock-out, relief cover | Quarterly review packs with minuted decisions; audit-response file; MAC appeal batch with invoices | PBM audit response filed on time with the attorney; MAC appeals batched, not one-by-one |
| 4 | **Professional simulation — the hard week** — PIC on leave (relief cover), a PBM desk audit arrives, a recall lands, and a wholesaler delivery fails, in the same week; run the absence-cover, audit-response, recall, and backup-wholesaler procedures for real | Perform under pressure with the procedures, not around them | Incident log, audit response, recall action record, backup-wholesaler order | Store never opens uncovered; recall actioned same day; audit response complete and on time |
| 5 | **Capstone — the quarterly business review the owner defends** — owner presents the quarterly pack (Q1) to the CPA and attorney: break-even position, margin cause, cash forecast, and one growth/stop decision, with numbers | Demonstrate independent mastery of the operating cycle | Signed quarterly review + annual plan (A1) | Every claim traced to a record; the decision is minuted with an owner and a date |
