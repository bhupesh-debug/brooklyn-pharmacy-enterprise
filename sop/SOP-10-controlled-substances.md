## Purpose

Order, store, dispense, count, and report controlled substances exactly as DEA and New York require — with a perpetual inventory that reconciles every day, PMP checks on every controlled-substance prescription, and loss/theft reported promptly — so that no controlled substance is ever unaccounted for.

## Scope

All controlled substances (Schedules II–V): DEA registration, ordering (CSOS), receiving, storage, dispensing, PMP/I-STOP reporting, perpetual and biennial inventories, recordkeeping, destruction, and loss/theft reporting. Covers the PIC's DEA power of attorney, delivery restrictions (with SOP-06), and the BNE position (SOP-08, step 7).

## Owner role (who is accountable)

- **Owner (accountable):** ensures the DEA registration is in the entity's name for this address, funds the safe/CCTV/perpetual-inventory system, and is notified of every variance, loss, or DEA/BNE contact the same day. The owner never handles controlled substances in a pharmacist capacity and never directs a pharmacist's controlled-substance judgment.
- **PIC (responsible):** owns the controlled-substance programme — ordering authority, receiving, perpetual inventory, PMP compliance, inventories, and loss/theft reporting.
- **Staff pharmacists:** dispense, verify, and check the PMP under the PIC's programme. Only a pharmacist holding the PIC's DEA power of attorney may order controlled substances besides the PIC.
- **RPhT / bookkeeper / driver:** never order, receive alone, reconcile alone, or dispense controlled substances. The bookkeeper never reconciles the controlled-substance perpetual inventory alone.

## Prerequisites

1. NYSED registration is issued for this address (DEA requires state authority first).
2. DEA registration (Form 224, online only) is issued for this entity and address — **$888/3 years** (21 CFR §1301.13; re-confirm the portal amount at filing). Renewal is Form 224a. DEA processing is commonly 4–6 weeks after state registration; no expedited processing for new applications.
3. The pharmacy's Health Commerce System (HCS) account and PMP/I-STOP access are set up and tested before opening, with 24-hour dispensing reporting configured in the PMS. HCS credentials are per person, never shared.
4. A securely locked cabinet/safe with pharmacist-only access, CCTV over the safe and dispensing area, and a perpetual-inventory system are in place.
5. The BNE position is confirmed in writing (SOP-08, step 7): a BNE (DOH-4330) licence is required only for specific activities such as operating an Automated Dispensing System at a long-term-care facility — confirm with BNE in writing before opening.
6. **[CONFIRM WITH COUNSEL/CPA]:** whether the MATE 8-hour training attestation applies to a pharmacy-entity DEA registration is UNVERIFIED in the master report — confirm with counsel/DEA before filing.

## Numbered steps

### Ordering

1. Order controlled substances only by the PIC or a pharmacist holding the PIC's DEA power of attorney, on the DEA-approved ordering system (CSOS electronic ordering). Record the order, the orderer, and the date.
2. For Schedule II, follow the DEA ordering rules in force on the day of the order (CSOS or Form 222 as applicable) — **[CONFIRM WITH COUNSEL/CPA]:** confirm the current Schedule II ordering procedure with the PIC/DEA at the time of the first order; do not rely on a remembered form.
3. Reconcile the order, the receipt, and the perpetual inventory the same day the delivery arrives (step 5).

### Receiving and storage

4. Receive controlled substances the same day they arrive. Check drug, strength, quantity, lot, and expiry against the invoice and the order. A second person checks high-value and controlled-substance receipts.
5. Enter the receipt into the perpetual inventory the same day. Reconcile the perpetual count to the delivery. Investigate any discrepancy the same day; log it and notify the PIC.
6. Store controlled substances only in the securely locked cabinet/safe, with pharmacist-only access. Keep Schedule II records separate and exact (see step 9).

### Dispensing

7. **Check the NY PMP/I-STOP record before dispensing every controlled-substance prescription.** Document the check, the result, and the pharmacist's decision in the PMS. Do not dispense a controlled substance until the PMP check is done.
8. **Schedule II: no refills.** Apply the strict quantity, dating, and record rules for Schedule II. Partial fills, transfers, and emergency fills follow the rules in force on the day — **[CONFIRM WITH COUNSEL/CPA]:** confirm the current Schedule II partial-fill/transfer rules with the PIC/counsel before relying on them.
9. **Final verification is pharmacist-only** (SOP-02). Count Schedule II exactly — inexact Schedule II counts are a leading DEA deficiency. Record the exact count.
10. **Report dispensing to the PMP within 24 hours** (I-STOP). Confirm the PMS transmission succeeded each day; if a transmission fails, notify the PIC the same day and re-transmit as soon as the system allows. Never share HCS credentials.
11. **Delivery of controlled substances** only under the PIC/counsel-approved rule (SOP-06, prerequisite 5). If no rule exists, do not deliver controlled substances.

### Inventories and recordkeeping

12. **Perpetual inventory:** every controlled-substance dispense, receipt, return, transfer, waste, and destruction moves the perpetual count the same day. Reconcile the perpetual inventory daily.
13. **Biennial inventory:** take a complete controlled-substance inventory at least every 2 years (21 CFR §1304.11) — and on the PIC's schedule if more frequent. A missed biennial inventory is the most-cited DEA deficiency. Diary the due date in the compliance calendar (SOP-08).
14. **Opening inventory:** on opening (or on an acquisition closing under a new DEA registration), take and record an opening controlled-substance inventory, with Schedule II on the applicable DEA form.
15. **Records:** keep controlled-substance records for at least 2 years (21 CFR Part 1304). Keep ordering, receipt, dispensing, perpetual, inventory, and destruction records separate and retrievable. Do not commingle controlled-substance records with general records where the rules require separation.

### Loss, theft, and destruction

16. **Investigate every variance the same week.** Any controlled-substance variance — however small — is investigated the same week, logged, and reported to the PIC. Never write off a controlled-substance variance without a documented investigation.
17. **Report loss/theft promptly (DEA Form 106).** On discovering a theft or significant loss, notify the PIC and the owner immediately and file DEA Form 106 promptly upon discovery — **[CONFIRM WITH COUNSEL/CPA]:** the commonly cited "1 business day" timing is UNVERIFIED against the current regulation text in the master report — confirm the current Form 106 timing with counsel/DEA and follow it. Notify BNE/NYSED as counsel requires. Never under-report a "small" loss.
18. **Destruction only with authorisation (DEA Form 41).** Destroy controlled substances only as authorised and documented. Never destroy a controlled substance without authorisation.

## Controls (what prevents error)

- Pharmacist-only ordering (PIC or DEA power-of-attorney holder), receiving with a second-person check, and dispensing.
- PMP check before every controlled-substance dispense; 24-hour PMP reporting with a daily transmission check.
- Exact Schedule II counts; perpetual inventory reconciled daily; biennial inventory diarised.
- Pharmacist-only safe access; CCTV over the safe and dispensing area; per-person HCS credentials, never shared.
- Zero tolerance for variances: same-week investigation, documented before any write-off.

## Failure modes (what goes wrong and the response)

| Failure | Response |
|---|---|
| Perpetual count does not match the safe count | Stop. Investigate the same week (same day if Schedule II). Check orders, receipts, dispensing, returns, and destruction. Notify the PIC. Do not write it off without a documented investigation. |
| PMP is unavailable at dispensing | Do not dispense a controlled substance until the PMP check can be done, unless the PIC directs otherwise under the rule in force and documents the decision. Log the outage. |
| PMP transmission fails | Notify the PIC the same day, re-transmit as soon as possible, and log the failure and the re-transmission. |
| Suspected diversion, forgery, or "doctor shopping" | Hold the prescription, check the PMP, escalate to the PIC the same day (SOP-09). Do not fill on assumption. |
| Theft or significant loss discovered | Notify PIC and owner immediately, preserve evidence, file Form 106 promptly per counsel-confirmed timing, and notify BNE/NYSED as counsel requires. |
| Biennial inventory due date approaching | PIC schedules and completes it before the due date. Never let it lapse — it is the most-cited DEA deficiency. |
| Controlled substance delivered without an approved rule | Stop controlled-substance deliveries immediately, notify the PIC, and log the incident. Put the PIC/counsel-approved rule in writing before resuming. |
